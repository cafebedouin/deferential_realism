# Constraint Testing Log

Use this template for systematic edge testing of claimed constraints.

## Purpose

This log helps you:
1. Test constraint claims systematically
2. Distinguish substrate from detritus from clarity fetishes
3. Track classification accuracy over time
4. Build your internal constraint library

#### Damage vs. Discomfort Check

Mark all that apply:

**Substrate Violation Indicators (DAMAGE):**
☐ Measurable physiological harm
☐ Cognitive function measurably declined
☐ Physical pain (sharp, not just discomfort)
☐ System failure (couldn't complete task due to capacity limit)
☐ Recovery required (needed time/resources to recover)

**Detritus Indicators (DISCOMFORT ONLY):**
☐ Felt uncomfortable but completed task
☐ Discomfort decreased over time during test
☐ No measurable decline in function
☐ No recovery needed after
☐ Discomfort was anticipatory (worse before than during)

**Clarity Fetish Indicators:**
☐ Acted without certainty → gained data
☐ Demand for certainty dissolved when dropped
☐ Uncertainty became tolerable through action
☐ Analysis paralysis broken by engagement

## Example Entry (For Reference)

### Test Entry #1

**Date:** 2026-01-24

**Constraint Claim:**
"I can't speak in public because I'll freeze and everyone will judge me."

**Initial Classification:** ☑ Detritus

**Evidence:**
1. Formed after one bad presentation in 7th grade
2. Haven't tested in 15 years
3. Circumstances completely different now

**When formed:** Age 12, middle school presentation
**Function then:** Avoid humiliation after teacher criticized me publicly
**Circumstances now:** Adult professional context, no authority figure to humiliate me
**Tested before:** No, always avoided

**Edge test:** Speak for 5 minutes at small team meeting (7 people)

**Safety bounds:** If actual panic attack, can stop. But try to distinguish discomfort from damage.

**Prediction:** Will feel uncomfortable but won't actually freeze

**Test performed:** Team meeting, presented quarterly results

**What happened:** 
- High anxiety beforehand (anticipatory)
- During: Slight shakiness, but spoke clearly
- Completed full 5 minutes
- No one reacted negatively
- Anxiety decreased during speaking

**Damage check:**
☑ Felt uncomfortable but completed task
☑ Discomfort decreased during test
☑ No measurable decline in function
☑ Discomfort was anticipatory

**Classification confirmed:** Detritus (clearable pattern)

**Lesson:** Fear was 90% anticipatory. Actual experience was manageable discomfort, not damage.

**Next test:** Speak at larger meeting (15 people)

**Calibration update:** This is NOT substrate. Continue testing progressively larger audiences.

## Tips for Effective Testing

### 1. Start Small
Test edges incrementally. Don't go from "never spoken" to "keynote speech."

### 2. Design Clear Tests
Make predictions specific enough to evaluate.

### 3. Watch for Damage Signals
Stop immediately if substrate violation occurs:
- Sharp pain
- Cognitive collapse
- Physiological emergency

### 4. Distinguish Discomfort from Damage
Discomfort is unpleasant feelings.
Damage is measurable harm.

### 5. Test Multiple Times
One test isn't conclusive. Pattern over time matters.

### 6. Document Honestly
Don't retrofit classification to match desire. Record what actually happened.

### 7. Update Calibration
As you clear detritus, your sense of what's possible expands. Track this.

### 8. Respect Substrate When Found
If testing reveals real damage, STOP. Defer to the substrate.

## Review Protocol

**Weekly:** Review all tests from the week
- What patterns are emerging?
- What's clearing faster than expected?
- What's proving to be substrate?
- Where is calibration improving?

**Monthly:** Review classification accuracy
- How many initial guesses were correct?
- Which type of constraint do you most misclassify?
- What's your success rate on edge testing?

**Quarterly:** Update constraint library
- Which detritus is now cleared?
- Which substrate is now accepted?
- What new constraints emerged?
