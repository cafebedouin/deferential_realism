# 🧭 Conflict Resolution Guide v2.0

**What this is:**  
A complete protocol for navigating interpersonal conflict—from immediate triage through active resolution to pattern diagnosis and repair.

**Who this is for:**  
People who have already completed Filter Alignment Check and know their blind-spot lens. This builds on that foundation.

**What this does:**  
Provides decision trees, protocols, and somatic checks for real-time conflict navigation. Not theory—practical tools for when things are going wrong.

---

## Understanding the Complete System

**This guide integrates four layers:**

**Layer 1: Filter Alignment** (Strategic)  
↓ Should I engage at all? Does benefit > resentment cost?

**Layer 2: Relationship Zone** (Diagnostic)  
↓ Is this Red/Yellow/Green? Community context?

**Layer 3: Structural Stability** (Tactical)  
↓ Which capacity do I need right now? (MIRROR, CHECK, CONTRARY, etc.)

**Layer 4: Conflict Protocol** (Operational)  
↓ Things went wrong. What's the next move?

**You are here:** Layer 4 (real-time navigation when conflict is active)

---

## Part 1: Immediate Triage (60 seconds)

**Before any conflict engagement, answer these three questions:**

### Question 1: What Zone Is This Relationship?

**Red Zone (0-10%):**
- Active harm, lies, manipulation, dignity violations
- **Action:** Exit protocol, not resolution
- **Skip to:** Part 7 (Emergency Exit)

**Yellow Zone (10-90%):**
- Messy, frustrating, but not deliberately harmful
- Most human relationships live here
- **Action:** Proceed to Question 2

**Green Zone (90-100%):**
- Trust, safety, mutual growth
- **Action:** Rare to have conflict here, but if so proceed to Question 2

**If unsure:** Use [Relationship Checklist](./relationship_checklist.md) - 7 quick questions

---

### Question 2: Do You Have Capacity Right Now?

**Check your body:**
- Can you feel your feet?
- Is your jaw clenched?
- Is your breath shallow?
- Do you feel flooding/freezing/urgency?

**Resourced (can practice):**
- Body relatively calm
- Can think clearly
- Not overwhelmed
- **Action:** Proceed to Question 3

**Depleted (will escalate):**
- Body contracted
- Thinking foggy
- Overwhelmed
- **Action:** Skip to Part 4 (Space Protocol)

**Violated (need safety):**
- Feel unsafe
- Dignity breached
- Fear response active
- **Action:** Skip to Part 7 (Emergency Exit)

---

### Question 3: What's Your Actual Goal?

**Understanding:**
- Want to understand what happened
- Want them to understand you
- Want shared reality
- **Action:** Proceed to Part 2 (Active Conflict Protocol)

**Winning:**
- Want to be right
- Want them to admit they're wrong
- Want to punish
- **Action:** Framework won't help. Either exit or acknowledge winning > understanding

**Exit justification:**
- Already decided to leave
- Looking for permission to exit
- Need them to be "bad enough"
- **Action:** Skip to Part 7 (Clean Exit Protocol)

**Containment:**
- Irresolvable values difference
- Need to manage, not resolve
- **Action:** Skip to Part 5 (Containment Protocol)

---

## Triage Decision Tree

```
Red Zone → Exit (Part 7)
Yellow Zone + Capacity + Understanding → Active Protocol (Part 2)
Yellow Zone + Depleted → Space (Part 4)
Yellow Zone + Capacity + Winning → Exit or Reframe
Yellow Zone + Capacity + Containment → Containment (Part 5)
Green Zone + Capacity + Understanding → Active Protocol (Part 2)
Any + Violated → Exit (Part 7)
```

## Part 2: Active Conflict Protocol (Yellow or Green Zone, Resourced, Want Understanding)

**This is the core protocol for navigating conflict in real-time.**

### The 6-Step Sequence

#### Step 1: PAUSE (Somatic Ground)

**Purpose:** Prevent escalation from reactive state

**The practice:**
- Take 2-3 deep breaths
- Notice body sensations
- Feel your feet on the floor
- Check: Can I stay present or am I in override?

**Somatic check:**
- ✅ Grounded: Jaw soft, breath flowing, can feel body
- ⚠️ Override: Jaw clenched, breath held, numb/contracted

**If override:** "I need a minute" → Step away → Return when regulated

**Time:** 30-60 seconds

---

#### Step 2: FRAME (Set Context)

**Purpose:** Signal intention and create container for hard conversation

**Sample language:**
- "Can we talk about something that didn't land well for me?"
- "I want to understand what happened—I'm confused and want clarity."
- "Something felt off. Can I check in about it?"

**What to avoid:**
- Accusation ("You did X wrong")
- Blame ("This is your fault")
- Ultimatum ("If you don't change...")

**Tone check:**
- Calm, not aggressive
- Curious, not certain
- Open, not closed

**If they're not ready:**
- "When would be a good time?"
- Don't force—return to Part 4 (Space)

**Time:** 30 seconds

---

#### Step 3: MIRROR (Reflect Their View)

**Purpose:** Verify understanding before responding

**The practice:**
- Repeat back what they said
- Check if you got it right
- Don't add interpretation
- Don't argue yet

**Format:**
- "So you're saying [X], is that right?"
- "Let me check if I understand: [restate their view]"
- "It sounds like [X]. Did I get that?"

**Watch for:**
- Do they relax slightly? (you understood)
- Do they say "No, that's not it"? (try again)
- Do they seem surprised you're listening? (good sign)

**Common mistakes:**
- Mirroring with sarcasm (performative, not genuine)
- Adding your interpretation ("So you're saying I'm wrong")
- Skipping to CONTRARY too fast (feels like you're not listening)

**From [Practitioner Quick-Start](./practitioner_quick_start.md):**
> MIRROR before CONTRARY = "You really understood me, then offered a different view"  
> CONTRARY without MIRROR = "You're not listening"

**Somatic check:**
- ✅ Genuine curiosity: Relaxed jaw/shoulders
- ⚠️ Performance: Tight chest, mechanical

**If you can't MIRROR genuinely:** You're depleted. Return to Part 4 (Space)

**Time:** 1-2 minutes

---

#### Step 4: CHECK (Test Assumptions)

**Purpose:** Expose hidden assumptions causing friction

**The practice:**
- Notice what you're assuming
- State it explicitly
- Ask if it's true

**Format:**
- "I'm assuming [Y], but maybe I'm wrong?"
- "I was thinking [X], is that how you see it?"
- "I noticed I assumed [Z]—did you mean that?"

**What assumptions to check:**
- Intent: "I assumed you were trying to hurt me"
- Meaning: "I thought you meant X, but maybe you meant Y?"
- Context: "I assumed you knew about [Z], but maybe not?"

**Watch for:**
- "Oh, no, actually..." (assumption revealed)
- "I didn't even think about that" (gap identified)
- Relief on their face (misunderstanding clarified)

**Common patterns:**
- Assuming malice instead of confusion
- Assuming they know what you know
- Assuming your interpretation is the only one

**From [Structural Stability Self-Check](./structural_stability_self_check.md):**
> "When something feels confusing, say 'I'm assuming [X], is that right?'"

**Somatic check:**
- ✅ Comfortable with uncertainty: Soft belly
- ⚠️ Anxious questioning: Held breath

**Time:** 1-2 minutes

---

#### Step 5: STATE (Your View, Not Theirs)

**Purpose:** Express your perspective clearly without demanding they change theirs

**The practice:**
- Use "I" statements
- Describe your experience
- Don't argue with their reality

**Format:**
- "Here's how it looked from my side: [X]"
- "I felt [Y] when [Z] happened"
- "My experience was different: [describe]"

**What to include:**
- What you observed (facts)
- How it affected you (feelings)
- What you need (boundary or request)

**What to avoid:**
- "You should have..." (prescriptive)
- "The right way is..." (absolute)
- "Anyone would feel..." (universal)

**Example:**
> "When you didn't respond to my text, I felt dismissed. I need acknowledgment when I share something hard. That's important to me."

**Not:**
> "You ignored me. You always do this. You should respond faster."

**Somatic check:**
- ✅ Clear statement from center: Steady voice
- ⚠️ Pushing hard: Locked jaw/knees

**Time:** 1-2 minutes

---

#### Step 6: FACTS (Ground in Shared Reality)

**Purpose:** Find common foundation to build from

**The practice:**
- List what both people know/agree on
- Separate facts from interpretations
- Build shared ground

**Format:**
- "What do we both know for sure?"
- "Can we agree on [X, Y, Z]?"
- "Here's what actually happened: [facts only]"

**Example:**
> "We both agree: You sent the email at 3pm. I didn't see it until the next day. We both wanted the project to succeed. Those are facts. Now let's talk about interpretations."

**Watch for:**
- Points of agreement emerging
- Tension decreasing as facts anchor
- Ability to separate "what happened" from "what it means"

**From [Filter Alignment](./filter_alignment_check.md):**
> If your Core Filter is TRUTH, this is your natural strength

**Common mistake:**
- Confusing interpretations with facts
- "The fact is you were being rude" (interpretation, not fact)
- "The fact is I was right" (opinion, not fact)

**Somatic check:**
- ✅ Grounded in observable reality
- ⚠️ Arguing about interpretation as if it's fact

**Time:** 1-2 minutes

---

### Success Markers (Things Are Working)

**You know the protocol is working when:**

✅ Tension decreases (not increases)  
✅ Both people feel heard (even without agreement)  
✅ Shared reality identified (foundation exists)  
✅ Bodies relax (can breathe, jaws soften)  
✅ Curiosity emerges (replacing defensiveness)  
✅ Misunderstandings clarified (wasn't what you thought)

**Outcome possibilities:**
- **Resolution:** Problem solved, repair achieved
- **Understanding:** Disagree but understand why
- **Containment needed:** Irresolvable difference identified → Part 5
- **Exit needed:** Not workable → Part 7

---

### Failure Markers (Abort Protocol)

**Stop immediately if:**

❌ Escalation continues despite lens use  
❌ Safety concerns emerge  
❌ Your capacity depletes (body goes into override)  
❌ They're actively manipulating/gaslighting  
❌ Pattern repeats 3+ times with no change  
❌ You realize you actually want to exit, not resolve

**Abort actions:**
- "I need space right now" → Part 4
- "This isn't working. I need to step back" → Part 7
- "I've lost capacity. We need to stop" → Part 4

## Part 3: Repair Protocol (After Escalation)

**Use when:** Conflict happened, things escalated, but relationship is viable (Yellow or Green Zone)

### Prerequisites for Repair

**Both people must be:**
- Back in window of tolerance (regulated)
- Willing to engage (not forced)
- Able to acknowledge what happened

**If not ready:** Wait. Attempting repair while still dysregulated will re-escalate.

**Timing:** Hours to days after rupture, not immediately

---

### The 5-Step Repair Sequence

#### 1. Acknowledge What Happened

**Don't minimize:**
- ❌ "It wasn't that bad"
- ❌ "Let's just forget it"
- ❌ "We were both wrong"

**Do name it:**
- ✅ "That conversation escalated"
- ✅ "I [raised voice, shut down, left abruptly]"
- ✅ "Things got heated and we both reacted"

**Purpose:** Reality acknowledgment, not blame distribution

---

#### 2. Take Responsibility (For Your Part)

**Not:**
- "I'm sorry IF you felt hurt" (conditional apology)
- "I'm sorry BUT you did X first" (deflection)
- "We're both to blame" (avoidance)

**But:**
- "I raised my voice. That wasn't okay."
- "I shut down instead of saying I needed space."
- "I made assumptions without checking."

**Own your actions specifically, not generically**

---

#### 3. Name Dignity Violations (If Any)

**Different from hurt feelings:**

**Dignity violation:**
- Contempt, ridicule, dismissal
- Name-calling, character attack
- Threats, coercion, power abuse

**Hurt feelings:**
- Disagreement, disappointment
- Misunderstanding, frustration

**If dignity violated:**
- "When you [X], that crossed a line for me"
- "That behavior isn't acceptable"
- "I need that to not happen again"

**This is boundary information, not blame**

---

#### 4. Request Repair (If Possible)

**Format:**
- "Can we try that conversation again?"
- "I'd like to understand what happened from your side"
- "Can we talk about how to handle this differently next time?"

**If they're not ready:**
- Accept that
- Don't force
- Revisit later or accept it stays ruptured

---

#### 5. Update the Protocol (Pattern Prevention)

**Questions:**
- What triggered escalation?
- What would have helped?
- What can we do differently next time?

**Build in:**
- Circuit breakers ("If X happens, we pause")
- Space requests ("I need 10 minutes" is valid)
- Pattern recognition ("This is the third time we've fought about Y")

**Example agreements:**
- "If either of us starts yelling, we take a 10-minute break"
- "If I need space, I'll say so instead of shutting down"
- "If you feel dismissed, you'll tell me right away"

---

### Repair Success Markers

✅ Both people feel heard about what went wrong  
✅ Specific changes identified (not vague "we'll try harder")  
✅ Relationship feels repaired (not just swept under rug)  
✅ Trust rebuilding possible (not permanently damaged)

---

### When Repair Isn't Possible

**Some ruptures don't repair:**
- Dignity violation too severe
- Pattern repeats despite agreements
- Trust broken beyond rebuilding
- One person won't acknowledge what happened

**If repair impossible after 2-3 attempts:**
- Accept the rupture
- Reduce contact or exit
- Don't keep trying to fix what can't be fixed

## Part 4: Space Protocol (Need Distance, Not Exit)

**Use when:** Depleted, overwhelmed, need time to regulate—but relationship is viable

### Immediate Space (Pause, Not Exit)

**When to use:**
- Capacity depleted during conflict
- Too angry/upset to think clearly
- Need processing time
- Prevent escalation

**The protocol:**

**1. Name the need clearly**
- "I need space right now"
- "I need to step away for a bit"
- "I can't continue this conversation right now"

**2. Specify timeline**
- "I'll be ready to talk in [X hours/days]"
- "Let's revisit this tomorrow"
- "I need the rest of today"

**Don't:**
- Leave indefinitely ("I need space" with no timeline)
- Punish with silence (silent treatment is manipulation)
- Explain what they did wrong (that's a different conversation)

**3. Actually use the space**
- Regulate your nervous system (walk, breathe, journal, move)
- Don't ruminate/rehearse arguments
- Don't vent to others without boundary
- Return to window of tolerance

**4. Return when ready**
- Honor your timeline
- Come back resourced
- Try Part 2 (Active Protocol) again

---

### Space Success Markers

✅ You return more regulated  
✅ Timeline honored (or renegotiated clearly)  
✅ Space used for regulation, not avoidance  
✅ Both people respect the need

**Space Failure Markers:**

❌ "Space" becomes indefinite ghosting  
❌ Used as punishment ("I'm taking space because you're bad")  
❌ Timeline broken repeatedly  
❌ Return still depleted (didn't actually regulate)

→ If space doesn't work, escalate to Part 7 (Exit)

---

## Part 5: Containment Protocol (Irresolvable Differences)

**Use when:** Fundamental values conflict that can't be resolved, only managed

### Recognition: This Is Containment Territory

**Signals:**
- Same argument repeats 3+ times
- Both people's positions are structurally legitimate
- No amount of MIRROR/CHECK reveals misunderstanding
- It's genuinely a values difference

**Examples:**
- Different risk tolerances (cautious vs. adventurous)
- Different priorities (stability vs. growth)
- Different core filters (truth vs. harmony, fairness vs. outcome)
- Different relationship to conflict (engage vs. avoid)

**From [Containment Framework](./containment_framework.md):** Some tensions are structurally permanent

---

### The 5-Step Containment Practice

#### 1. Name the Paradox

**Format:**
- "We both value X AND Y, but they're in tension"
- "You need [A], I need [B], and we can't both get 100%"
- "This is a real conflict with no perfect solution"

**Example:**
> "You need predictability and routine. I need spontaneity and flexibility. These are both legitimate needs, and they're genuinely in tension."

---

#### 2. Accept Irresolvability

**Hard truth:**
- This isn't solvable
- One person surrendering their values isn't resolution
- The tension is structural, not personal failure

**What this is NOT:**
- Resignation ("nothing matters")
- Giving up ("why try")
- Defeat ("we failed")

**What this IS:**
- Wisdom ("some things can't be resolved, only held")
- Maturity ("I can live with permanent tension")
- Reality ("not everything has a solution")

---

#### 3. Negotiate Alternation or Boundaries

**Alternation (Sequential Satisfaction):**
- "Your priority this week, mine next week"
- "Routine on weekdays, spontaneity on weekends"
- "You get final say on X, I get final say on Y"

**Boundaries (Partial Zones):**
- "In this domain, we do it your way"
- "In that domain, we do it mine"
- "We each maintain autonomy in our respective areas"

**Hybrid (Oscillation):**
- "We try it your way for 3 months, evaluate, then my way for 3 months"
- "We alternate who leads on major decisions"

---

#### 4. Define Non-Negotiables

**Each person identifies:**
- Where's the line?
- What's absolutely not okay?
- What would require exit?

**Example:**
> "I can alternate on most things, but I need [X] to be non-negotiable. If that can't be honored, this relationship doesn't work for me."

**This creates clarity:** Here's the zone of acceptable containment. Outside this, we exit.

---

#### 5. Monitor Stability Over Time

**Check-ins (monthly):**
- Is alternation sustainable?
- Are boundaries holding?
- Is tension contained or escalating?
- Can we both live with this long-term?

**If containment is stable:**
- Continue pattern
- Accept ongoing tension
- Don't demand resolution

**If containment is failing:**
- One person's needs chronically unsatisfied
- Resentment overwhelming any benefit
- Boundaries repeatedly violated
- **Action:** Escalate to Part 7 (Exit)

---

### Containment Success Markers

✅ Both people acknowledge the paradox  
✅ Sustainable alternation or boundaries established  
✅ Relationship stable despite irresolvable difference  
✅ No one's trying to "fix" the other person  
✅ Comfort with incompleteness

**From [Containment as Paradox](./containment_as_paradox.md):**
> Maturity is holding permanent tension without demanding resolution

---

## Part 6: Pattern Diagnosis (Why Does This Keep Happening?)

**Use when:** Same conflict recurs 3+ times despite attempts to resolve

### Diagnostic Framework

**Ask:** What's the actual pattern here?

#### Pattern 1: Mismatched Core Filters

**Recognition:**
- You optimize for different things
- What matters to you doesn't matter to them
- Both positions are structurally legitimate

**Example:**
- You (TRUTH filter): "We need to understand reality correctly"
- Them (HARMONY filter): "We need to keep the peace"
- Every conflict: You push for clarity, they push for smoothing over

**From [Filter Alignment](./filter_alignment_check.md):**
> Core Filters are axiomatic—they rarely change

**Solution:**
- Make filters explicit ("I care about truth, you care about harmony")
- Negotiate alternation ("Sometimes truth priority, sometimes harmony priority")
- Or accept incompatibility → Part 7 (Exit)

---

#### Pattern 2: Relationship Zone Mismatch

**Recognition:**
- You think this is Yellow Zone (workable)
- They think this is Red Zone (harmful)
- OR: You think Green Zone (trust), they think Yellow (messy)

**Example:**
- You: "This is normal relationship messiness"
- Them: "This is violating my dignity"

**From [Relation Zone Guide](./relation_zone_guide.md):**
> Red Zone = Get away. Yellow Zone = Try improvement. Green Zone = Protect.

**Solution:**
- Explicit zone conversation ("Where is this relationship for you?")
- If zones don't align, exit likely
- Can't force someone to stay in Yellow when they experience Red

---

#### Pattern 3: Community Context Undermining Repair

**Recognition:**
- Individual relationship is Yellow Zone
- But community is Red Zone (toxic environment)
- Every repair attempt gets sabotaged by environment

**Example:**
- You and coworker get along fine
- But workplace culture punishes honesty
- Every attempt to resolve conflict gets weaponized by others

**From [Relation Zone Guide](./relation_zone_guide.md):**
> "If you live in a bad community, even good people will start acting badly"

**Solution:**
- Change environment (leave toxic community)
- Create protective bubble (limit community interference)
- Or accept that repair impossible in this context

---

#### Pattern 4: Unacknowledged Power Asymmetry

**Recognition:**
- One person has more power (boss/employee, parent/child, financially dependent)
- Conflict protocol assumes equal footing
- Power difference makes "negotiation" impossible

**Example:**
- Employee tries to resolve conflict with boss
- Boss can fire employee
- "Mutual repair" isn't actually mutual

**From [Conflict Resolution v1.6](./conflict_resolution_guide.md):**
> "If there's significant risk of retaliation, do not proceed without safety"

**Solution:**
- Name asymmetry explicitly
- Adjust expectations (lower power can't demand equal say)
- Or exit if power imbalance makes relationship untenable

---

#### Pattern 5: Violated Boundary Not Enforced

**Recognition:**
- You state boundary ("Don't raise voice")
- They violate it
- You continue relationship without consequence
- Pattern repeats

**Why it keeps happening:**
- Boundary without enforcement is just a request
- They learn boundary isn't real
- Pattern continues

**From [Boundaries Guide](./boundaries_guide.md):**
> Boundaries require enforcement, not just statements

**Solution:**
- Actually follow through on stated consequence
- "If you raise voice again, I'm ending this conversation" → Then actually do it
- Or accept you won't enforce → Not a real boundary

---

#### Pattern 6: Resource Depletion Cycle

**Recognition:**
- You're consistently practicing from depleted state
- No capacity to use lenses
- Every conflict engagement escalates because you're not resourced

**Why it keeps happening:**
- Trying to resolve while exhausted
- No space between conflicts to recover
- Chronic depletion becomes pattern

**Solution:**
- Build in space BEFORE conflict likely
- Don't engage when depleted
- Increase baseline capacity (sleep, support, boundaries)
- Or exit if relationship chronically depletes you

---

#### Pattern 7: Fundamental Incompatibility (Exit Condition)

**Recognition:**
- None of the other patterns fit
- Relationship is structurally extractive
- All your energy goes to managing their chaos
- Repair attempts consistently fail
- Pattern has repeated 5+ times

**From [Filter Alignment](./filter_alignment_check.md):**
> "Is the strategic benefit greater than the resentment cost?"

**If benefit < cost consistently:**
- This isn't a pattern to fix
- This is a relationship to exit
- **Action:** Part 7 (Clean Exit)

---

## Part 7: Exit Protocols (Relationship Should End)

**Use when:** Dignity repeatedly violated, fundamental incompatibility, harm outweighs benefit

### Three Types of Exit

#### A. Temporary Boundary (Pattern Needs Addressing)

**Use when:**
- Same violation keeps recurring
- Need consequence to enforce boundary
- Relationship might be viable IF pattern stops

**The protocol:**

**1. Name the pattern**
- "This has happened [X] times now"
- Be specific, not vague

**2. State boundary**
- "I can't keep having this conversation without [Y changing]"
- Make it behavioral, not emotional

**3. Specify consequence**
- "If it happens again, I'll [reduce contact/end relationship/etc.]"
- Be clear and realistic

**4. Follow through**
- If pattern persists, actually do what you said
- Don't threaten repeatedly without action
- Builds trust OR clarifies exit needed

**Example:**
> "You've raised your voice in three of our last conversations. I need that to stop. If it happens again, I'm taking a month of no contact. If it continues after that, I'm ending the friendship."

---

#### B. Gradual Reduction (Slow Exit)

**Use when:**
- Can't do sudden exit (family, coworker)
- Need to phase out contact
- Want to test if distance helps

**The protocol:**

**1. Reduce frequency**
- Weekly → Monthly
- Daily texts → Occasional check-ins
- Regular hangouts → Special occasions only

**2. Shorten duration**
- 3-hour visits → 1-hour
- Long conversations → Brief check-ins

**3. Limit depth**
- Don't share vulnerable things
- Keep surface-level
- Reduce emotional investment

**4. Create space**
- Say no more often
- Be less available
- Prioritize other relationships

**Watch for:**
- Does distance make relationship better? (maybe sustainable at low dose)
- Does distance make you feel relief? (exit confirmation)
- Do they respect the boundary? (good sign) or push back? (bad sign)

---

#### C. Clean Exit (Relationship Ends)

**Use when:**
- Dignity repeatedly violated
- Fundamental incompatibility clear
- Pattern won't change
- Harm outweighs benefit
- You're done

**The protocol:**

**1. You don't need their agreement**
- Exit is unilateral decision
- Their understanding is nice but not required
- Don't wait for permission

**2. You don't need to make them understand**
- They may never "get it"
- Explaining endlessly doesn't help
- Accept they'll have their version of events

**3. State it clearly once**
- "This relationship isn't working for me. I'm ending it."
- "I need to reduce contact to zero."
- "I'm stepping back from this friendship."

**4. Actually exit**
- Don't threaten repeatedly without following through
- Don't leave door open if you mean closed
- Don't engage in endless "final conversations"

**5. Manage social fallout**
- Mutual friends may take sides
- They may tell their story differently
- You can't control their narrative
- State your boundary to others if needed

**Example:**
> "I've decided to end our friendship. I'm not interested in processing this further. I wish you well, but I need to move on."

---

### Exit Success Markers

✅ You actually exit (don't keep engaging)  
✅ Relief > guilt (confirmation it was right)  
✅ Capacity frees up for healthier relationships  
✅ Pattern stops (because you're not in it anymore)

**Exit Failure Markers:**

❌ Keep threatening but don't follow through  
❌ Leave but keep checking in  
❌ Exit with resentment/revenge (not clean)  
❌ Feel guilty for having boundaries

---

### Emergency Abort Conditions (Exit Immediately)

**Stop ALL protocol attempts if:**

🚨 Physical safety threatened  
🚨 Dignity violation escalating  
🚨 Coercion, manipulation, gaslighting continues  
🚨 Pattern repeated 5+ times with zero change  
🚨 Your core filter violated repeatedly  
🚨 You're practicing from override (body shutdown)  
🚨 Relationship structurally harmful

**Remember:** You don't owe anyone your continued presence in harm.

## Part 9: Common Mistakes and Corrections

### Mistake 1: Using Lenses as Performance, Not Practice

**What it looks like:**
- Mechanically saying "So you're saying..." (MIRROR)
- But body is contracted
- Not actually curious
- Just going through motions

**Why it fails:**
- Other person senses inauthenticity
- You're in override, not capacity
- Performance creates more friction

**Correction:**
- Pause when body contracts
- Return to capacity before continuing
- If can't get to genuine state, use Part 4 (Space)

**From [Structural Stability](./structural_stability_self_check.md):**
> If your body is contracted while using a lens, you're practicing from override

---

### Mistake 2: Trying to Fix Red Zone People

**What it looks like:**
- Someone lies, manipulates, violates dignity
- You think "if I just use the right lens..."
- Keep trying to resolve what can't be resolved

**Why it fails:**
- Red Zone = Harm, not messiness
- Protocol assumes good faith
- Framework can't fix bad actors

**Correction:**
- Accept Red Zone classification
- Use Part 7 (Exit) immediately
- Stop trying to resolve the unresolvable

**From [Relation Zone Guide](./relation_zone_guide.md):**
> Red Zone: Just leave. Don't try to fix it.

---

### Mistake 3: Confusing Containment with Avoidance

**What it looks like:**
- Use "containment" as excuse to never address conflict
- "It's just a paradox" becomes "I won't talk about it"

**Why it fails:**
- Genuine containment requires active holding
- Avoidance is just deferred explosion
- Pattern worsens over time

**Correction:**
- True containment = Named paradox + Negotiated alternation + Monitored stability
- Avoidance = Unnamed tension + No structure + Hope it goes away
- If you're avoiding, go to Part 2 (Active Protocol)

---

### Mistake 4: Practicing When Depleted

**What it looks like:**
- Try to resolve conflict when exhausted
- Can't access lenses
- Escalation happens
- Blame self for "failing"

**Why it fails:**
- Capacity is prerequisite
- Depletion = Override likely
- Not moral failure, structural reality

**Correction:**
- Check capacity FIRST (Part 1, Question 2)
- If depleted, use Part 4 (Space)
- Build baseline capacity outside conflict times

---

### Mistake 5: Expecting Reciprocity

**What it looks like:**
- "I did MIRROR, why won't they?"
- "This isn't fair, I'm doing all the work"
- Resentment builds

**Why it fails:**
- Framework assumes asymmetry (from Filter Alignment)
- If you expect reciprocity, filter misaligned
- Resentment means benefit < cost

**Correction:**
- Revisit [Filter Alignment](./filter_alignment_check.md)
- Check: Is benefit actually > resentment for YOU?
- If no, exit is valid choice
- If yes, accept asymmetry as strategic cost

**From [Filter Alignment](./filter_alignment_check.md):**
> Can you tolerate the asymmetry? If "only if they eventually reciprocate" → Misaligned

---

### Mistake 6: Ignoring Community Context

**What it looks like:**
- Focus only on individual relationship
- Ignore toxic environment
- Wonder why repair always fails

**Why it fails:**
- Bad community undermines good relationships
- Environment matters as much as individual
- Can't fix relationship in toxic soil

**Correction:**
- Assess community zone (Part 6, Pattern 3)
- Ask: Is environment the problem?
- Consider: Would this relationship work in different context?

**From [Relation Zone Guide](./relation_zone_guide.md):**
> Good relationships need good soil to grow in

---

### Mistake 7: Forcing Resolution of Fundamental Incompatibility

**What it looks like:**
- Keep trying to "fix" what's actually exit condition
- Pattern repeats 5+ times
- Still hoping it will change
- Resentment overwhelming

**Why it fails:**
- Some relationships should end
- Not all conflicts are resolvable
- Staying past exit point is harm

**Correction:**
- Accept Pattern 7 (Fundamental Incompatibility)
- Use Part 7 (Clean Exit)
- Release hope that this specific relationship will work

## Appendix: Real-World Application Example

**Scenario:** Friend repeatedly cancels plans last minute (5th time)

**Part 1: Triage**
- Zone: Yellow (frustrating, not harmful)
- Capacity: Yes (annoyed but not dysregulated)
- Goal: Understanding (want to know what's going on)
- **Decision:** Proceed to Part 2

**Part 2: Active Protocol**

**PAUSE:** Take 3 breaths, notice irritation, feel feet, stay present

**FRAME:** "Hey, can we talk about the cancellations? I'm confused and want to understand what's happening."

**MIRROR:** "So it sounds like you've been overwhelmed with work stuff lately, is that right?"  
[They confirm]

**CHECK:** "I was assuming you didn't care about our plans, but maybe that's not it?"  
[They clarify: Not that they don't care, genuinely overwhelmed]

**STATE:** "I value our friendship and I'm feeling hurt by the pattern. I need reliability in friendships."

**FACTS:** "We've made plans 5 times in last 2 months. You've cancelled day-of each time. That's the pattern."

**Outcome:** Understanding achieved. They're in crisis period at work. Not about me.

**Part 5: Containment** (if needed)
"I hear you're overwhelmed. Can we do monthly instead of weekly until work calms down? That way we set realistic expectations."

**OR Part 7: Exit** (if pattern continues)
"I've realized I need more consistency than you can offer right now. I'm going to step back from this friendship."

**Key insight:** Same scenario, multiple valid paths depending on context and needs.
