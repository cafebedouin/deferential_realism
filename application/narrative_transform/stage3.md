\### ATTRACTOR TARGET (Required Explicit Decision)



Before selecting voice archetype, identify which terminal attractor the constraint logic will reach:



□ \*\*Deterministic Tragedy\*\*

&nbsp; - Constraints run to completion, no escape

&nbsp; - All rational actors follow index logic → inevitable collapse

&nbsp; - Perfect institutional rationality (optimize without bounds)

&nbsp; - Emotional core: Recognition without escape

&nbsp; - Compatible voice archetypes: The Condemned, The Witness

&nbsp; - Reader experience: "This is why things don't change"



□ \*\*Negotiated Equilibrium\*\*

&nbsp; - Constraints find balance point through rational bargaining

&nbsp; - Bounded institutional rationality (satisfice under uncertainty)

&nbsp; - Costs remain real, movement is incremental

&nbsp; - Emotional core: Recognition with limited agency

&nbsp; - Compatible voice archetypes: The Builder, The Negotiator

&nbsp; - Reader experience: "This is how we make things change, sometimes, barely"



□ \*\*Revolutionary Rupture\*\*

&nbsp; - Constraint logic itself disrupted by exogenous shock

&nbsp; - System-level transformation, not character-level

&nbsp; - Radical uncertainty about new equilibrium

&nbsp; - Emotional core: Disorientation and possibility

&nbsp; - Compatible voice archetypes: The Survivor, The Prophet

&nbsp; - Reader experience: "This is when the rules stop applying"



□ \*\*Seeded Possibility\*\*

&nbsp; - Surface tragedy with underground transformation

&nbsp; - Characters lose but ideas/practices spread invisibly

&nbsp; - Long-term structural change via cultural transmission

&nbsp; - Emotional core: Defeat with legacy

&nbsp; - Compatible voice archetypes: The Condemned, The Teacher

&nbsp; - Reader experience: "They lost, but something survived"



\*\*CRITICAL:\*\* Attractor selection must be \*\*compatible with constraint logic\*\*:

\- Mountains (■C) limit which attractors are reachable

\- Tangled Ropes (⊞⊠C) favor Negotiated Equilibrium or Seeded Possibility

\- Pure Snares (⊠C) favor Deterministic Tragedy or Revolutionary Rupture

\- Index variance determines whether negotiation is structurally possible

\- If all constraints are Mountains → only Deterministic Tragedy or Seeded Possibility

\- If key constraint is Tangled Rope → Negotiated Equilibrium becomes possible

\- If exogenous shock is specified → Revolutionary Rupture becomes possible

\## PRIMARY VOICE ARCHETYPES



\### □ The Condemned

\*\*Description:\*\*

\- Character is inside the constraint logic, cannot see systemic shape

\- Acts rationally given limited information and structural position

\- Experiences constraint as natural/inevitable, not constructed

\- Produces tragedy without moral failure or villainy

\- Voice is immediate, experiential, not reflective



\*\*Narrative Characteristics:\*\*

\- Limited temporal horizon (can't see long-term patterns)

\- Embedded in daily survival, not strategic thinking

\- Constraint errors feel reasonable given knowledge state

\- Reader may see pattern before character does

\- Ending: Recognition comes too late or not at all



\*\*Attractor Compatibility:\*\*

\- ✓ \*\*Deterministic Tragedy\*\* (primary match)

\- ✓ \*\*Seeded Possibility\*\* (character loses but legacy persists)

\- ✗ Negotiated Equilibrium (requires strategic capability)

\- ✗ Revolutionary Rupture (requires system-level perspective)



\*\*Requires:\*\*

\- PIR institutional model OR Mountain-dominated constraint set

\- Type I or Type III errors as natural given character's index

\- Power position: Powerless or Moderate (π ≥ 1.0)



\*\*Genre Affinity:\*\*

\- Tragedy (Metamorphosis, Odd John)

\- Social realism with inevitable decline

\- Noir without detective agency

\- Historical fiction with structural determinism



\*\*Warning Signs You've Chosen Wrong:\*\*

\- Character suddenly "gets it" and starts organizing

\- Character begins explaining constraint logic to others

\- Character finds leverage points and uses them strategically

\- Story needs to pivot to hope but can't because voice won't allow it



\*\*Example Sentences:\*\*

\- "The air had been thin for three years now. Nobody remembered when they stopped noticing."

\- "He'd thought the contract would end. His father's father had thought the same thing."

\- "The foreman's clipboard meant everything. She didn't need to see what he wrote."

\### □ The Builder

\*\*Description:\*\*

\- Character sees partial system shape, tests for leverage points

\- Acts strategically within constraints, learns from failures

\- Understands some rules can be changed, works to identify which

\- Experiences setbacks but maintains agency

\- Voice is tactical, observational, adaptive



\*\*Narrative Characteristics:\*\*

\- Medium temporal horizon (sees patterns, plans iterations)

\- Balances survival needs with strategic positioning

\- Makes Type IV errors sometimes (destroys functional coordination)

\- Reader experiences discovery process alongside character

\- Ending: Achieves limited but real structural change



\*\*Attractor Compatibility:\*\*

\- ✓ \*\*Negotiated Equilibrium\*\* (primary match)

\- ✓ \*\*Revolutionary Rupture\*\* (secondary—if equipped for chaos)

\- ⚠ Seeded Possibility (possible but voice may be too hopeful)

\- ✗ Deterministic Tragedy (creates tonal dissonance)



\*\*Requires:\*\*

\- BIR institutional model (negotiation must be structurally possible)

\- Tangled Rope or Snare as key constraint (not Mountain)

\- Power position: Moderate or Powerful (0.6 ≤ π ≤ 1.0)

\- Crisis mechanism or information asymmetry character can exploit



\*\*Genre Affinity:\*\*

\- Political thriller with structural wins

\- Heist/caper with systemic stakes

\- Organizing narratives (labor, community)

\- Hard SF with problem-solving emphasis



\*\*Warning Signs You've Chosen Wrong:\*\*

\- Character keeps strategizing but nothing ever works (voice promises agency but structure denies it)

\- Institutional capitulation feels unearned (BIR model not properly justified)

\- Victory feels hollow because costs weren't real (avoided actual compromise)

\- Reader expects negotiation but story delivers only tragedy



\*\*Example Sentences:\*\*

\- "The third time they cut rations, she started tracking the timing. Patterns meant options."

\- "If the overseer needed their expertise during the crisis, that was a card to play later."

\- "Small wins first. Prove value. Build credibility. Then push for structural terms."

\## COMMON VOICE SELECTION ERRORS



\### Error: The Hopeful Condemned

\*\*Problem:\*\* Selecting "The Condemned" voice but Negotiated Equilibrium attractor

\*\*Result:\*\* Character gains strategic capability that voice can't support

\*\*Fix:\*\* Either switch to Builder voice OR switch to Tragedy attractor



\### Error: The Passive Builder

\*\*Problem:\*\* Selecting "The Builder" voice but Deterministic Tragedy attractor

\*\*Result:\*\* Character strategizes but nothing works, voice promises agency that doesn't exist

\*\*Fix:\*\* Either switch to Condemned voice OR switch to Equilibrium attractor



\### Error: The Invisible Witness

\*\*Problem:\*\* Selecting "The Witness" voice but failing to justify observer position

\*\*Result:\*\* Voice feels like authorial intrusion, not character perspective

\*\*Fix:\*\* Create structural justification (journalist, researcher, bureaucrat with access)



\### Error: The Overexplaining Guide

\*\*Problem:\*\* Selecting "The Guide" voice but explaining too much

\*\*Result:\*\* Framework becomes visible, story becomes essay

\*\*Fix:\*\* Reduce meta-commentary, let more emerge through scene



\### Error: The Arbitrary Prophet

\*\*Problem:\*\* Selecting "The Prophet" voice but no justification for prophetic knowledge

\*\*Result:\*\* Feels like authorial cheating

\*\*Fix:\*\* Provide mechanism (prior experience, specialized knowledge, mystic tradition)

\### EQUILIBRIUM STABILITY VALIDATION (If Attractor = Negotiated Equilibrium)



Before proceeding to Stage 4, verify:



□ \*\*Mutual Benefit Check\*\*

&nbsp; - What does institutional index gain? (Be specific, not vague "good PR")

&nbsp; - What does powerless index gain? (Substantive, not symbolic)

&nbsp; - Would both choose this deal again in 5 years?



□ \*\*Defection Prevention\*\*

&nbsp; - What stops institution from defecting later?

&nbsp; - Ongoing monitoring/enforcement mechanisms?

&nbsp; - Iterative relationship or one-shot deal?



□ \*\*Realistic Compromise\*\*

&nbsp; - Both sides sacrifice something

&nbsp; - Terms preserve core interests of both

&nbsp; - Ongoing tension remains (not "happily ever after")



□ \*\*Crisis Justification\*\* (if crisis mechanism used)

&nbsp; - Crisis severity proportionate to institutional risk-taking

&nbsp; - Capability demonstrated under pressure, not just claimed

&nbsp; - Post-crisis payoff matrix genuinely different



\*\*RED FLAGS (If any of these, equilibrium is unstable):\*\*

\- Institution suddenly "becomes nice" without structural reason

\- Protagonist wins without institutional gaining anything

\- No ongoing enforcement mechanism

\- Crisis feels contrived/deus ex machina

\- Deal too favorable to one side
