# Stage 0: Indexed Constraint Extraction

---

## CHARACTER INDICES

### Spencer Brydon (Protagonist)

- **Power:** moderate → powerful (π = 1.0 → 0.6)
  He is a wealthy property owner with independent income from New York rents, yet experiences himself as having squandered agency. His power is economic but not social or psychological; he cannot leverage it to resolve his central dilemma.
- **Time horizon:** biographical (lifetime — obsessed with what his life *could have been*)
- **Exit options:** mobile (can return to Europe at any time, has chosen to stay)
- **Scope:** local (face-to-face encounters; his crisis is entirely personal and site-specific)
- **Index:** I_B = (moderate-to-powerful, biographical, mobile, local)

**Note:** Brydon's effective power is paradoxical. He has economic mobility but is psychologically trapped. Where it matters most—against the central constraint—his wealth and mobility count for nothing. For economic/social constraints he operates at π = 0.6. For the psychological constraints that drive the plot, he functions closer to π = 1.0 or even 1.5.

---

### Alice Staverton

- **Power:** moderate (π = 1.0)
  Economically constrained ("delicately frugal"), but possesses a rare capacity for perception and emotional steadiness. Her power is analytical and relational, not institutional.
- **Time horizon:** biographical (lifetime, but with patience that extends toward generational stillness)
- **Exit options:** constrained (she has clung to her small house, her life is narrow but chosen)
- **Scope:** local (σ = 0.8) — face-to-face relationships, intimate knowledge
- **Index:** I_S = (moderate, biographical, constrained, local)

**Note:** Staverton occupies an analytical position (π = 1.15 for detection purposes) relative to Brydon's crisis. She sees the alter ego in dreams, recognizes what it is, and does not flinch. Her constraint experience differs from Brydon's primarily in that she can perceive without being consumed.

---

### The Alter Ego (The Unlived Self / The Ghost)

- **Power:** powerless-to-powerful (oscillates)
  Within the house, the alter ego has a kind of territorial power — it closes doors, holds ground, advances. But it exists only as a function of Brydon's obsession; it has no independent agency outside the psychic economy of the haunting.
- **Time horizon:** biographical (it is the condensation of 33 years of unlived alternative)
- **Exit options:** trapped (it cannot leave the house or the logic that generated it)
- **Scope:** local (exists only within the jolly corner)
- **Index:** I_AE = (powerless/powerful, biographical, trapped, local)

**Note:** The alter ego is structurally unusual — it is both the *product* of a constraint and a *constraint itself*. It functions as what the protagonist's obsession generates when pressed to its logical conclusion.

---

### Mrs. Muldoon (Minor — Institutional Baseline)

- **Power:** powerless (π = 1.5)
- **Time horizon:** immediate
- **Exit options:** constrained (employed, dependent)
- **Scope:** local (σ = 0.8)
- **Index:** I_M = (powerless, immediate, constrained, local)

**Note:** Mrs. Muldoon provides the baseline experiential contrast. She refuses to enter after dark. Her classification of the house as dangerous is correct from her index and functions as a calibration point for the reader.

## CONSTRAINT INVENTORY WITH INDEXICAL VARIANCE

---

### C1: Irreversibility of biographical choice

The 33-year absence from America and the developmental path not taken. The life Brydon actually lived — expatriate, aesthete, possibly dissolute — versus the life he might have lived as an American man of business. This choice, once made, compounded over decades into a divergence that cannot be undone.

**Classification by index:**

- **■C1[I_B]:** Mountain from Brydon's index. He cannot go back to age 23 and choose differently. The divergence is temporal and biological — no amount of money or will can reverse it. ε ≈ 0, enforcement is zero because it is a fact of lived time.

- **■C1[I_S]:** Mountain from Staverton's index as well. She acknowledges the irreversibility freely: "It hasn't spoiled your being here at last." She routes around it rather than fighting it.

- **■C1[I_AE]:** Mountain from the alter ego's index. The ghost exists *because* this divergence is irreversible. If the choice could be undone, the alter ego would have no function. It is the Mountain's shadow.

**Measurements:**
- X_base(C1) ≈ 0
- ε(C1) ≈ 0 (no enforcement needed — time's arrow)
- β(C1, Brydon): Net cost = the permanent loss of the alternative self
- β(C1, Staverton): Net cost = modest (she has made her peace)
- Δ(C1): 0 degrees of freedom from any index. True Mountain.

**Transformation:** None. Mountains do not transform. But Brydon's *response* to this Mountain generates C2.

---

### C2: The obsessive pursuit of the unlived self

Brydon's compulsion to haunt his own house nightly, stalking an alter ego he believes he can confront. This begins as curiosity, escalates into ritual, and becomes an all-consuming practice that structures his entire existence in New York. He prowls from dusk to 2 AM, developing cat-like perceptual acuity, reading the house's signals as a hunter reads a forest.

**Classification by index:**

- **⊞⊠C2[I_B]:** Tangled Rope from Brydon's index. The obsession provides *genuine* coordination: it organizes his return to America, gives shape to his days, activates dormant capacities (his "fine attention, never in his life before so fine"), and connects him to his family past. But it simultaneously extracts: it isolates him socially (his public life becomes "mere surface sound"), it escalates beyond his control, and it drives him toward a confrontation that nearly kills him.
  - χ(C2, I_B) = ε(0.55) × π(1.0) × σ(0.8) = 0.44 → Tangled Rope
  - Coord(C2) = true (genuine self-knowledge pursuit, activated capacities)
  - Asymmetric(C2) = true (costs escalate while benefits plateau)

- **⊠C2[I_S]:** Borderline Snare from Staverton's index — not because she is subject to it, but because she watches it consume someone she loves. She sees the extraction clearly ("you don't care for anything but yourself"), recognizes it as dangerous, and yet does not intervene directly. From her analytical position:
  - χ(C2, I_S) = ε(0.55) × π(1.15) × σ(0.8) = 0.506 → Tangled Rope (analytical detection)
  - She perceives both the coordination function (Brydon's genuine need to know) and the extraction (it is devouring him)

- **⊞C2[I_AE]:** Rope from the alter ego's index. The obsession *is* the mechanism by which the alter ego gains presence. From its position, the pursuit is pure coordination — it is being called into existence, given form, offered encounter. The alter ego does not experience extraction; it *is* the extraction.

**Measurements:**
- X_base(C2) = 0.55
- ε(C2) = 0.55 (moderate-high base extraction — the obsession demands enormous time and psychic energy)
- Supp(C2) = 0.35 (self-enforced — Brydon is both jailer and prisoner)
- β(C2, Brydon): Benefit = activated perception, self-confrontation; Cost = isolation, escalating terror, near-death
- β(C2, Staverton): Benefit = none direct; Cost = anxiety, exclusion from his inner life
- Δ(C2, Brydon): He could stop at any time (mobile exit options) but *won't* — the compulsion functions as self-imposed entrapment

**Transformation:**
- Initial: ⊞C2[I_B] — Rope (curiosity, "wanton wonderment," the "quaint analogy")
- Trigger: The first fortnight's intensification; the sensation of being followed
- Transition: ⊞C2 → ⊞⊠C2 (extraction accumulates: nightly vigils, escalating stakes)
- Later trigger: The night of the closed door; the decision to confront
- Final state: ⊞⊠C2 → ⊠C2 (extraction dominant: terror, near-fatal collapse)
- Resolution: Brydon's surrender ("I retire, I renounce") attempts to cut the constraint but does not prevent the final confrontation

---

### C3: The two properties as competing models of selfhood

The "apartment-house" being reconstructed into flats (modern, profitable, vulgar) and the jolly corner kept empty and unchanged (ancestral, sentimental, sacred). Brydon discovers an unexpected talent for construction management at the first property while refusing to develop the second. The two buildings literalize two possible selves: the businessman and the aesthete, the American and the expatriate.

**Classification by index:**

- **⊞⊠C3[I_B]:** Tangled Rope from Brydon's index. The property system provides genuine coordination: income, social identity, a reason to be in New York, and an arena for discovering latent capacities ("a capacity for business and a sense for construction"). But it also extracts: the apartment-house forces him into the "vulgar and sordid" world of commerce, while the jolly corner becomes the site of his dangerous obsession.
  - χ(C3, I_B) = ε(0.40) × π(0.6) × σ(0.8) = 0.192 → Rope (from his wealthy position, property is net coordination)
  - But the *psychological* extraction is not captured by economic power-scaling. The jolly corner's emptiness is a psychic cost the formula can't reach.

- **⊞C3[I_S]:** Rope from Staverton's index. She sees the properties clearly as practical assets and psychological metaphors. She can admire the jolly corner without being consumed by it, and she notes with "mild irony" that the sky-scraper profits will fund his sentimentality. Net coordination, minimal extraction.

- **⊞C3[I_M]:** Rope from Mrs. Muldoon's index — the jolly corner is her workplace. She opens windows, dusts, sweeps. The property system is a Rope that provides employment. Her only extraction: the expectation she might be asked to come after dark (which she refuses, correctly identifying C2's extraction signature leaking into her workspace).

**Measurements:**
- X_base(C3) = 0.40
- ε(C3) = 0.25 (moderate — property ownership requires maintenance, decisions, engagement)
- β(C3, Brydon): Economic benefit + identity framework; Cost = entanglement with both commerce and haunting
- Δ(C3, Brydon): High degrees of freedom — he could sell either property. His *refusal* to sell the jolly corner is a choice, not a constraint. This makes C3 a vehicle for C2 rather than an independent binding force.

**Transformation:**
- The jolly corner transitions from Rope (shelter, heritage, income) to the *site* of C2's extraction. The building itself doesn't change, but its function in Brydon's psychic economy shifts from coordination to extraction.

### C4: The demand for self-knowledge versus the cost of confrontation

The core dramatic constraint. Brydon wants to know what he would have become. But the knowledge, when it arrives, is unbearable — the alter ego is a stranger, "unknown, inconceivable, awful, disconnected from any possibility." The pursuit of self-knowledge turns out to be a pursuit of something that cannot be integrated. The face behind the hands is not his face.

**Classification by index:**

- **■C4[I_B]:** Mountain from Brydon's index — but a *false* Mountain. Brydon experiences the confrontation as an encounter with something unchangeable and alien. The alter ego's strangeness feels like a fact of nature. But the "Mountain" is actually generated by his own obsession (C2) and his error in treating his unlived life as a recoverable object. The apparent immutability is constructed.
  - FM detection: ε(C4) is high — the confrontation requires enormous enforcement energy (the entire apparatus of nightly stalking). True Mountains require no enforcement. This is a False Mountain.
  - Underlying type: ⊠C4[I_B] — Snare. The confrontation extracts (terror, collapse, near-death) without coordinating (the "knowledge" gained is pure negation: "it's not me"). χ(C4, I_B) = ε(0.70) × π(1.5*) × σ(0.8) = 0.84 → Snare
  - *Note: At the moment of confrontation, Brydon's effective power drops to powerless. His wealth, mobility, and social position mean nothing against the apparition. π shifts to 1.5.

- **⊞C4[I_S]:** Rope from Staverton's index. She has already seen the alter ego in dreams — twice — and accepted it. "I had accepted him." For her, the confrontation with the unlived self is a coordination mechanism: it produces knowledge, compassion, integration. She pities the alter ego. She is not destroyed by what Brydon cannot bear.
  - χ(C4, I_S) = ε(0.70) × π(1.15) × σ(0.8) = 0.644 → Tangled Rope, but she processes the extraction through acceptance rather than resistance, functionally reducing it to Rope
  - Her analytical capacity transforms the experience

- **⊠C4[I_AE]:** Snare from the alter ego's index. The confrontation is extractive for the alter ego as well — it is rejected, denied, called "a black stranger." It gained form only to be disowned. Its hands cover its face in "dark deprecation," not aggression. When it drops its hands, the result is mutual destruction: Brydon collapses, and the alter ego presumably dissipates.

**Measurements:**
- X_base(C4) = 0.70 (high base extraction — this is knowledge that damages)
- ε(C4) = 0.70 (requires massive energy to produce: weeks of stalking, one climactic night)
- Supp(C4) = 0.85 (self-enforcement at extreme levels)
- β(C4, Brydon): Cost = near-fatal psychic collapse; Benefit = eventual integration (but only through Staverton's mediation, not direct experience)
- β(C4, Staverton): Cost = anxiety, vigil; Benefit = confirmation of her love, acceptance of both selves
- Δ(C4, Brydon): Brydon had the chance to refuse (the closed door — "I retire, I renounce"). He chose discretion, but the confrontation found him anyway at the vestibule. Degrees of freedom narrowed to zero at the terminal moment.

**False Mountain analysis:**
Brydon treats the alter ego as an alien, unchangeable fact — "it's not me." This is Type I error: treating a constructed apparition (product of his obsession, his property, his history) as an independent, Mountain-like reality. Staverton's correction is precise: "Isn't the whole point that you'd have been different?" The strangeness of the alter ego is not a Mountain; it is the *logical consequence* of the divergence (C1, which IS a Mountain). Brydon conflates the derivative with the source.

**Transformation:**
- Initial: The pursuit of self-knowledge appears as Rope (curiosity, meaning-making)
- Through C2's escalation: Transitions to Tangled Rope (real insight + real cost)
- At confrontation: Fully Snare (pure extraction — knowledge that cannot be integrated)
- Post-collapse (through Staverton): Recovers to Rope — she provides the interpretive frame that makes the knowledge bearable. "You came to yourself."

---

### C5: The social world as performance versus the private world as authentic

Brydon's public life in New York — dining out, clubs, renewing acquaintances — is described as pure theater: "mere surface sound," "extravagant shadows," a "game of ombres chinoises." His real life begins when he enters the jolly corner. This constraint bifurcates his existence into false public self and "true" private obsession.

**Classification by index:**

- **⊟C5[I_B]:** Piton from Brydon's index. Social performance has lost all coordination function for him. It provides no genuine connection, no information, no resource. But it persists — he continues attending, performing, being "positively rather liked than not." Theater(C5) ≥ 0.70. It costs maintenance energy (time, attention) but delivers nothing.
  - χ(C5, I_B) = ε(0.15) × π(1.0) × σ(0.8) = 0.12 → Piton range (low extraction, high theater)

- **⊞C5[I_S]:** Rope from Staverton's index. For her, social life is genuine — she has "communities of knowledge," her small scene of "items and shades, all delicate things." Her social world coordinates because it remains honest and scaled to human capacity. She doesn't perform; she cultivates.

**Measurements:**
- X_base(C5) = 0.15 (low extraction — social life takes time but not much else)
- Theater(C5) = 0.80 from Brydon's index (almost entirely performance)
- Theater(C5) = 0.20 from Staverton's index (mostly substance)

**Indexical tension:** Brydon treats social performance as dead weight (correctly, from his index — it IS Piton for him). But he uses this to validate his obsessive private life as the "real" one. The error is not in classifying C5 as Piton, but in treating the *alternative* (C2, the nightly stalking) as its opposite — authentic engagement — when C2 is itself Tangled-to-Snare.

## ERROR STRUCTURE WITH INDEXICAL NOTES

---

### Type I Error (False Mountain): Brydon treats the alter ego's strangeness as an encounter with alien reality

**Description:** When Brydon sees the face — mutilated hand, ruined sight, "unknown, inconceivable, awful" — he recoils as if encountering a Mountain: something that simply IS, independent of him. "A thousand times yes, the face was the face of a stranger." He treats the difference as a natural law rather than a product of divergent circumstances acting on shared material.

**Indexical note:** The alter ego's strangeness IS a Mountain from one angle — the irreversibility of time (C1) means this alternative genuinely cannot be reached. But the *face itself* is not a Mountain; it is the output of C1 plus American life plus 33 years of a different path. Brydon's error is collapsing the derivative (the specific face) with the source (the irreversible divergence), then treating his horror as a discovery about reality rather than a revelation about his own assumptions.

**Staverton's correction:** "Isn't the whole point that you'd have been different?" She identifies the Type I error directly. Of course the alter ego is a stranger — that was the premise. Brydon's shock reveals that he never actually believed in the difference; he expected to find himself, slightly modified. His obsession (C2) was built on the implicit assumption that the alter ego would be recognizable. The Mountain he encounters is the one he was already standing on.

**Consequence chain:** Horror → collapse → near-death → rescue by Staverton → slow re-integration via her interpretive frame.

---

### Type II Error (Mountain Denial): Brydon's belief that the unlived life can be recovered through confrontation

**Description:** The entire structure of the haunting assumes that if Brydon can *see* the alter ego, he can *know* what he missed. This is Mountain Denial — treating the irreversibility of biographical time as something that can be overcome through sufficient intensity of encounter. The nightly stalking is an attempt to reverse entropy through pure attention.

**Indexical note:** From Brydon's index, this error is understandable. He has the wealth and mobility (high E) to do almost anything else he wants. The one thing he cannot do is undo his choice. His error is applying the logic of his economic freedom (where problems yield to resources) to a temporal constraint (where they do not).

**Consequence:** Massive energy depletion. Weeks of nightly prowling. Escalating terror. Ultimate collapse — the exact pattern predicted by Type II error: fighting a Mountain until energy is exhausted.

---

### Type III Error (Missing Extraction): Brydon treats the obsession as pure self-knowledge pursuit

**Description:** Brydon frames his nightly haunting as an adventure, a sport, a "preposterous secret thrill." He compares himself to a Bengal tiger hunter, a "monstrous stealthy cat." He experiences the obsession as coordination — a mechanism for self-discovery, for activating dormant capacities, for connecting with his ancestral past. He misses, or suppresses, the extraction: the isolation, the escalation beyond his control, the progressive transfer of agency from himself to the presence he pursues.

**Indexical note:** The obsession genuinely IS partially coordination (C2 is Tangled, not pure Snare). Brydon is not entirely wrong. His error is Type III — treating a Tangled Rope as a pure Rope, seeing the coordination while minimizing the extraction. The thrill is real. So is the damage.

**Consequence:** Continued participation despite escalating costs. The classic Type III outcome: he keeps engaging because the coordination benefits (excitement, meaning, purpose) are real, while the extraction (terror, isolation, energy depletion) accumulates silently until the system breaks.

---

### Type V.a Error (Tangled-as-Rope): Brydon's relationship with the apartment-house

**Description:** Brydon discovers he has a gift for construction management and enjoys the work. He treats this as pure coordination — evidence of dormant talent, a surprise pleasure. He does not examine whether the "capacity for business and a sense for construction" he has discovered is the very quality he has been hunting in his alter ego. The apartment-house is where he IS the American businessman, but he treats this as play rather than recognition.

**Indexical note:** Staverton sees it: "If he had but stayed at home he would have anticipated the inventor of the sky-scraper." Her irony identifies the Tangled nature — his business talent is evidence of the alter ego's reality, which means the pursuit (C2) is both more justified and more dangerous than he realizes.

## INDEXICAL CONFLICT MAP

---

### Core Tension: C4 (Confrontation with the unlived self)

**Brydon experiences as:** Snare (extraction without usable coordination — the knowledge destroys rather than informs)

**Staverton experiences as:** Rope (she accepts the alter ego, pities him, integrates the knowledge — "I had accepted him")

**The Alter Ego experiences as:** Snare (called into existence only to be rejected — its deprecating posture suggests it knows what's coming)

**Dramatic structure:**

1. Brydon pursues self-knowledge (C2 as Tangled Rope — genuine benefits + genuine costs)
2. Pursuit escalates beyond control (C2 transitions toward Snare)
3. Confrontation arrives (C4 — False Mountain for Brydon, Rope for Staverton)
4. Brydon cannot integrate what he finds (Type I error — treats derivative as source)
5. Collapse (energy depletion from Type II error — fought the Mountain)
6. Staverton provides interpretive frame that transforms C4 from Snare back to Rope
7. Resolution: "You came to yourself" — the self-knowledge *was* gained, but only through another's mediation

**The structural irony:** Brydon's entire apparatus of solitary confrontation — the nightly prowling, the hunter's patience, the cultivation of perception — was the wrong tool. Self-knowledge, in this system, cannot be extracted through individual heroic encounter. It requires another index: Staverton's analytical, compassionate, non-adversarial position. What is Snare when approached alone becomes Rope when approached through relationship.

## CONSTRAINT INTERACTION PATTERNS

---

- **C1 (irreversibility) generates C2 (obsession):** The Mountain's existence creates the compulsive desire to see what lies on its other side.
- **C2 (obsession) colonizes C3 (properties):** The jolly corner shifts from Rope (heritage) to the site of C2's extraction. The apartment-house provides ironic counterpoint.
- **C2 (obsession) produces C4 (confrontation):** When C2 escalates to maximum extraction, C4 becomes inevitable — the system must resolve.
- **C4 (confrontation) triggers C1 (irreversibility) as experienced truth:** The encounter with the alter ego makes the Mountain *felt* rather than merely known.
- **C5 (social performance) enables C2:** Brydon's social life provides cover for the nightly haunting. Its Piton status (dead theater) means no one notices or intervenes.
- **Staverton breaks the C2→C4→collapse cascade** by offering an alternative index through which C4 functions as Rope rather than Snare.

**Dominant cascade for Brydon:**
C1 (Mountain) → C2 (Tangled → Snare) → C4 (False Mountain / Snare) → collapse

**Staverton's counter-cascade:**
C1 (Mountain, accepted) → dream-knowledge (Rope — she already saw the alter ego) → C4 (Rope — acceptance) → rescue and reinterpretation

## TERMINAL ATTRACTOR

---

### Without Staverton:

The system converges toward **psychic death or literal death.** Brydon himself perceives this: seeing the open vestibule door would "send him straight about to the window" — i.e., defenestration. The terminal attractor of the C2→C4 cascade, unmediated, is self-destruction. Energy depletion (Type II error — fighting a Mountain) combined with Snare extraction (C4 confrontation) leaves him no resources to recover.

**Convergence mechanism:**
- C1 (Mountain) blocks the only exit Brydon wants (undoing the past)
- C2 (Tangled→Snare) drains energy asymmetrically across weeks
- Type I error (False Mountain at confrontation) prevents integration
- Type II error (Mountain Denial in the obsession) prevents course correction
- Energy depletion until collapse — the only remaining state

### With Staverton:

The system converges toward **integration through relationship.** Staverton's index (analytical, accepting, already in possession of dream-knowledge) provides an alternative processing path. She transforms C4 from Snare to Rope not by changing the constraint but by providing a different structural position from which to experience it.

**From Brydon's index:** Terminal state = collapse, rescue, dependence, slowly rebuilt self-knowledge
**From Staverton's index:** Terminal state = confirmation of love, acceptance of both selves, "And now I keep you"

**Differential equilibria:** What is near-death for Brydon is confirmation for Staverton. The same outcome (the confrontation happened, the alter ego was seen, Brydon collapsed) means collapse from one index and fulfillment from another. Same event, different structural positions, different experienced meanings.

**The final exchange encodes the indexical resolution:**

Brydon: "He has a million a year. But he hasn't you."
Staverton: "And he isn't — no, he isn't — you!"

The Mountain (C1) stands. The divergence is real. The alter ego is genuinely a stranger. But the system's terminal attractor is not destruction — it is the discovery that self-knowledge requires a second index, that the Mountain can be lived with rather than conquered, and that the Tangled Rope of obsession, once cut, leaves behind something it was not designed to produce: mutual recognition.
