The following table compares the seat distribution for the 163 members of the Council of Paris under the original PLM (Paris-Lyon-Marseille) law and the new electoral reform (Loi Maillard) scheduled for the 2026 municipal elections.

### Comparison of Seat Allocation (163 Total Seats)

| Feature | Old System (PLM Law) | New Reform (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Electoral Scale** | **Sector-based**: 17 separate elections (one per arrondissement). | **City-wide**: One single direct election for the whole city. |
| **Majority Bonus %** | **50% of seats** awarded to the leading list in each sector. | **25% of seats** awarded to the list that comes first city-wide. |
| **Fixed Bonus Seats** | **82 seats** (approximate city-wide total from sector wins). | **41 seats** (exactly one quarter, rounded up). |
| **Proportional Seats** | **81 seats** (approximate city-wide total). | **122 seats** (remaining three quarters). |
| **Threshold for Seats** | 5% of votes cast **within the sector**. | 5% of votes cast **across the entire city**. |
| **Impact on Majority** | Favors a strong, dominant majority; difficult for small lists. | Encourages political diversity; requires more coalition-building. |

### Key Structural Changes

1. **Shift to Direct Universal Suffrage:** Previously, the Mayor of Paris was elected indirectly. Voters chose district councilors who then formed the Council of Paris. Under the new reform, voters cast a direct ballot for the Council of Paris list, which then elects the Mayor, making the link between the vote and the executive more direct.
2. **Reduction of the Majority Premium:** By lowering the winner's "bonus" from 50% to 25%, the system shifts away from a "winner-takes-most" model. While the 50% rule almost guaranteed a governing majority for the leading coalition, the 25% rule means that even the winning list will likely need to form alliances with smaller parties to reach the 82-seat majority required to govern.
3. **City-wide Representation:** The old system led to "demographic distortions" where a vote in a small arrondissement carried different weight than a vote in a large one. The 2026 reform treats all 1.3 million Parisian voters as a single constituency for the City Council, ensuring that seats are distributed based on the total number of votes received across the entire city.

This transition increases the weight of proportional representation, significantly lowering the barrier for third-place and fourth-place lists to gain seats in the Council, while potentially making the formation of a stable executive more complex.
