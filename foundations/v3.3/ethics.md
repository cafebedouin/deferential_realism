# Deferential Realism: Ethics

**Living Consistently With What You Actually Believe About Constraints**

**Version 3.3 - Integrated**

---

## I. Foundation: The Examined Constraint-Set

The central ethical question in Deferential Realism is not "What should I believe about constraints?" but **"Do I know what I actually believe about the constraints I face?"**

This is a Socratic ethics: the unexamined belief-set is the primary ethical failure. Not because there's one correct set of beliefs about constraints, but because **arguments only bind you if they rest on axioms you actually accept**.

### The Bhagavad Gita Example

In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna argues that Arjuna should fight in the war. The argument rests on several axioms:
- Atman (immortal soul) exists
- Reincarnation is real
- Dharma (caste duty) transcends individual preference
- The Kshatriya warrior duty is legitimate

**If you accept these axioms:** Krishna's argument is sound. Fighting doesn't harm the eternal essence, so Arjuna's reluctance comes from confusion about what's actually at stake. The constraint is a Mountain (metaphysical necessity) that Arjuna must accept.

**If you reject these axioms:** Krishna's argument is a Snare. He's using metaphysical claims Arjuna doesn't believe to extract military service in a war Arjuna wants to avoid.

**The ethical requirement:** You must examine which you actually believe. Not which sounds profound, not which tradition endorses, not which authority proclaims—but which you can honestly affirm as true about reality.

**This framework cannot tell you which to believe.** It can only insist that you examine the question, because the entire ethical analysis depends on it.

### The Ulysses Lesson

In James Joyce's *Ulysses*, Stephen Dedalus has perfect clarity about the constraints binding him. He knows he's trapped between British Empire and Catholic Church. He understands the extraction happening. He sees all the frames clearly.

**And the clarity doesn't free him.**

The lesson: Classification clarity is **necessary but not sufficient** for effective action. You need:

1. **Know what the constraint is** (epistemology - covered in epistemology.md)
2. **Know what you believe about it** (examined belief - this document's core)
3. **Choose appropriate response** (conditional virtues - see below)
4. **Accept that clarity doesn't eliminate constraint** (wisdom - sometimes there is no exit)

Understanding constraint structure doesn't eliminate the constraints. Sometimes clarity just reveals that your freedom lies in **choosing your orientation** within constraints you cannot change. This is wisdom, not salvation.

## II. The Conditional Virtues

These are not universal moral duties. They are **practices that serve specific values**. If you share the values, the practices are useful. If you don't share the values, these virtues may not apply to you.

**Classification provides information. Values determine response.** The framework maps terrain; your values choose the route.

### Virtue 1: Acceptance (For Those Who Value Effective Agency)

**The Value:** Effective agency within actual limits (not wasting finite energy fighting unchangeable reality)

**The Practice:** Accurately identify unchangeable constraints (Mountains) and redirect energy from impossible fights to tractable problems.

**Conditional statement:** IF you value accomplishing things within your lifetime, THEN accurately distinguishing what cannot be changed from what can be changed prevents wasted energy.

**Examples:**

**Mountains (genuinely unchangeable):**
- Thermodynamic limits (cannot create energy from nothing)
- Biological requirements (humans need oxygen, water, sleep)
- Logical necessity (cannot have contradictions be true)
- Historical facts (cannot change what has already happened)
- Your starting position (born in specific place, time, circumstances)

**Not-Mountains (often misclassified - False Summits):**
- "Human nature" claims about hierarchy, competition, gender roles (often constructed, not biological)
- "Economic laws" about market necessity (often policy choices, not physical laws)
- "Traditional values" about family structure, sexual behavior (vary across cultures and time)

**The practice:**
- When you encounter claimed necessity, ask: "What's the evidence this is unchangeable?"
- Test: If enforcement stopped, would it persist? (Mountains persist, Ropes/Snares collapse)
- Historical test: Has this been different in other times/places?
- Counterfactual test: Can you imagine solving the problem differently?
- **Discomfort vs. Damage test:** Does violation produce subjective fear or objective harm?

**The strategic benefit:** Accepting Mountains immediately frees energy for actual problems. The climber who accepts gravity finds the route. The one who denies it dies.

**The failure mode:** Treating changeable constraints as unchangeable (false fatalism) leads to unwarranted surrender of agency. But this is a **strategic error** (wastes opportunities), not a moral failure.

**Why this is conditional:** Some people value acceptance over accomplishment, devotion over effectiveness, faith over pragmatic success. For them, this virtue may not apply. The framework doesn't claim everyone should prioritize effective agency.

### Virtue 2: Resistance (For Those Who Value Autonomy)

**The Value:** Autonomy and freedom from extraction (not having your life energy harvested by others)

**The Practice:** Identify extractive structures (Snares) that masquerade as natural necessity, and respond strategically (resist when you have power, exit when you don't, expose when you can).

**Conditional statement:** IF you value autonomy and dislike being extracted from, THEN distinguishing genuine constraints from manufactured ones enables strategic response.

**The diagnostic:**
- **Beneficiary analysis:** Who wins systematically? Who loses?
- **Snap-back test:** Remove enforcement—does it collapse immediately or persist?
- **Implementation gap:** Does practice diverge from stated purpose?
- **Naturalization rhetoric:** Does it claim "this is just how things are"?
- **Alternative suppression:** Are viable alternatives blocked?

**Examples:**

**External Snares (political economy):**
- Rent-seeking regulations serving narrow interests (claimed necessary, actually extractive)
- Artificial scarcity through IP extension far beyond incentive needs
- "Required" credentials that serve gatekeeping, not quality
- Policies that concentrate wealth while claiming efficiency
- Payday loans, predatory contracts, non-compete clauses

**Internal Snares (personal extraction):**
- Addiction (claims necessity, actually drains agency)
- Toxic relationships (claim love, actually extract energy)
- Self-destructive habits rationalized as "who I am"
- Beliefs that serve anxiety rather than truth
- Clarity Fetishes (demands for impossible precision/comfort - see psychology.md)

**The practice:**
- Name the beneficiary (including when it's your own fear/comfort)
- Distinguish extraction from coordination
- Resist when you have power; exit when you don't
- Build alternatives (Scaffolds, new Ropes) before cutting if load-bearing
- Expose naturalization fraud (challenge False Summit claims)

**Power-indexed obligation:** Response depends on your power position:
- **Powerless:** Focus on exit strategies, harm reduction, building collective power
- **Moderate:** Organize with others, expose extraction, push for reform
- **Powerful:** Dismantle extraction, refuse complicity, redirect power
- **Institutional:** Redesign systems, eliminate extraction mechanisms

**The strategic benefit:** Identifying Snares enables appropriate resistance. You stop wasting energy maintaining structures that extract from you.

**The failure mode:** Seeing extraction everywhere (paranoia) or seeing it nowhere (naivety). Both are **strategic errors**, not moral failures.

**The partisan commitment:** This framework is explicitly anti-authoritarian on one point: **power masquerading as natural law is fraudulent**. Naming something as a Snare is taking a political position—you're challenging its legitimacy. This is intended, not accidental.

But: This doesn't mean all power is fraudulent. Coordination requires power. Enforcement serves functions. The question is: Does this power serve extraction or coordination? And that question requires evidence, not ideology.

**Why this is conditional:** Some people value hierarchy, tradition, or security over autonomy. For them, resisting extraction may be less important than maintaining order. The framework doesn't claim everyone should prioritize autonomy over all other values.

**Terminology note:** Previously called "Noose," now "Snare" for climbing metaphor consistency (trap in terrain).

### Virtue 3: Reform (For Those Who Value Both Coordination AND Justice)

**The Value:** Preserving collective benefit while eliminating extraction

**The Practice:** Identify hybrid structures (Tangled Ropes) and surgically separate coordination function from extraction mechanism.

**Conditional statement:** IF you value both effective coordination AND freedom from extraction, THEN learning to reform hybrids without destroying coordination is essential.

**Recognition:** 36% of analyzed constraints show this irreducible hybrid structure—both coordinate AND extract. Neither pure Rope nor pure Snare.

**Examples:**

**Tangled Ropes (coordination + extraction):**
- Carbon credit markets (climate coordination + financial rent extraction)
- Health insurance systems (risk pooling + administrative extraction)
- Academic publishing (knowledge sharing + access rent)
- Social media platforms (connection + attention/data extraction)
- Venture capital (startup funding + control extraction)

**The diagnostic:**
- Does this solve genuine coordination problem? (YES)
- Does this also extract asymmetrically? (YES)
- Can functions be separated? (ASSESS)

**The practice:**

**Step 1: Identify Coordination Core**
- What problem does this solve?
- Would problem persist without this mechanism?
- Can coordination be verified independently?

**Step 2: Measure Extraction Layer**
- Who gains from extraction vs. coordination?
- What's ratio of coordination benefit to extraction cost?
- Is extraction structurally necessary or grafted on?

**Step 3: Test Separability**
- Can coordination persist with extraction removed?
- Do alternative systems exist that coordinate without extraction?
- Historical cases: Where has extraction been removed? What happened?

**Step 4: Strategic Reform**
- If separable: Preserve coordination, excise extraction
- If inseparable: Assess whether tradeoff is acceptable or full replacement needed
- If uncertain: Run controlled experiments

**The strategic benefit:** Reform preserves collective capacity while eliminating harm. You don't throw coordination baby out with extraction bathwater.

**The failure modes:**
- **Treat as pure Rope:** Defend extraction, maintain harm
- **Treat as pure Snare:** Destroy coordination, lose collective benefit
- **Attempt reform without power:** Get captured by defenders of extraction

**Power-indexed obligation:**
- **Powerless:** Push for reform while protecting yourself from extraction
- **Moderate:** Organize reform coalitions, build alternative models
- **Powerful:** Fund experiments, create space for alternatives
- **Institutional:** Redesign from position of control

**Why this is conditional:** Some value revolutionary purity over incremental reform (prefer to dismantle even if coordination is lost). Others value stability over justice (prefer to maintain even if extraction continues). This virtue serves those who want both coordination AND fairness.

**This is the hardest virtue.** Easier to accept everything (Acceptance) or reject everything (Resistance). Reform requires distinguishing genuine from fraudulent, preserving while excising, strategic precision.

### Virtue 4: Construction (For Those Who Value Coordination)

**The Value:** Coordination and mutual benefit (enabling collective action without coercion)

**The Practice:** Build and maintain mechanisms (Ropes) that are transparent, reciprocal, and revisable.

**Conditional statement:** IF you value enabling cooperation without extraction, THEN designing transparent coordination mechanisms serves this goal.

**Two domains:**

**Personal Ropes (Self-Coordination):**
- Habits as "contracts with future self"
- Routines that solve present-self/future-self conflicts
- Commitment devices that align short-term impulses with long-term values
- Example: Automatic savings = Rope preventing present-self from robbing future-self

**Interpersonal/Institutional Ropes (Social Coordination):**
- Agreements that prevent collision and enable cooperation
- Standards that make joint action possible
- Rules that manage genuine scarcity fairly
- Institutions that solve coordination problems at scale

**The practice:**
- Design mechanisms that serve participants, not just designers
- Make rules transparent (no hidden agendas)
- Build in revision mechanisms (assume conditions will change)
- Test: "Does this enable agency or stifle it?"
- **Monitor for extraction accumulation** (Rope → Tangled Rope degradation)

**The reciprocity test:** An ethical Rope benefits participants approximately equally (accounting for different needs). If someone consistently gains while others consistently lose, it's become a Tangled Rope or Snare, not a Rope.

**The strategic benefit:** Good Ropes enable things impossible alone (language, traffic laws, money, technical standards). They create collective capacity.

**The failure mode:** Building opaque Ropes (mechanisms that hide their true function) or rigid Ropes (cannot adapt when conditions change). These are **strategic errors**—they're planting future problems, not moral failures per se.

**Why this is conditional:** Some people value individual freedom over collective coordination, spontaneity over system, or prefer to navigate without rules. For them, building coordination mechanisms may feel like unnecessary constraint. The framework doesn't claim everyone should value coordination over individual freedom.

### Virtue 5: Maintenance (For Those Who Value Adaptation)

**The Value:** Adaptation to changing conditions (not wasting energy on obsolete structures)

**The Practice:** Regular audit and retirement of structures that no longer serve their original function (Pitons).

**Conditional statement:** IF you value adapting to changing conditions rather than maintaining tradition for its own sake, THEN regularly retiring obsolete structures conserves energy.

**Recognition:** Pitons are degraded constraints—once served coordination, now high-cost with low benefit.

**The recognition:**
- Rules that solved yesterday's problems but not today's
- Habits useful in past contexts but now counterproductive
- Beliefs accurate for previous conditions but outdated
- Institutions serving functions that no longer exist

**Examples:**
- Fax requirements in email era
- Obsolete regulatory reporting
- Dead letter laws still on books
- Legacy system dependencies
- Zombie committees (meet but produce nothing)
- Skeuomorphic design elements

**The practice:**
- **Scheduled audits:** Review personal habits quarterly, institutional rules annually
- **Environmental scanning:** Notice when context shifts
- **Decay detection:** Distinguish "no longer useful" from "never was useful"
- **Cost-benefit assessment:** Does maintenance cost exceed any remaining benefit?
- **Clean deletion:** Remove rather than rationalize-and-keep

**The strategic benefit:** Pitons waste energy with no payoff. Every hour spent on vestigial processes is an hour not spent on actual problems.

**The failure mode:** Deleting too quickly (discarding wisdom) or never deleting (calcifying tradition). Both are **strategic errors**.

**The sunk cost principle:** Keeping something because you invested energy building it (when it no longer serves) compounds the waste. Acknowledge the loss, delete the piton, redirect saved energy.

**Why this is conditional:** Some people value tradition, continuity, and stability over adaptation. For them, maintaining practices even when conditions change may serve important identity or community functions. The framework doesn't claim everyone should prioritize adaptation over tradition.

**Terminology note:** Previously called "Zombie," now "Piton" for climbing metaphor (old anchor that fails under load).

### Virtue 6: Containment (For Those Who Value Maturity)

**The Value:** Wisdom to hold irresolvable tensions without forcing collapse

**The Practice:** Recognize structural paradoxes (overhangs) and navigate them through sequential alternation, not simultaneous optimization or forced resolution.

**Conditional statement:** IF you value psychological maturity and effective long-term navigation, THEN learning to contain rather than resolve structural tensions is essential.

**Recognition:** Some tensions are structurally permanent. Both poles valuable, mutually incompatible, forcing resolution causes collapse.

**Examples of structural paradoxes:**
- **Autonomy ⇄ Connection** (both needed, always in tension)
- **Security ⇄ Growth** (both valuable, trade-offs inevitable)
- **Present ⇄ Future** (current satisfaction vs. future preparation)
- **Material frame ⇄ Functional frame** (both true, both incomplete)

**The practice (from psychology.md):**

1. **Name** the paradox (what two goods are in tension?)
2. **Map** the oscillation (how does tension manifest?)
3. **Test** edges (is resolution actually impossible or just uncomfortable?)
4. **Accept** irresolvability (this is structure, not failure)
5. **Choose** for current context (partial satisfaction on one side)
6. **Hold** tension without collapse (don't force resolution)

**What containment is NOT:**
- Resignation ("nothing matters")
- Optimization ("maximize both")
- Resolution ("I figured it out")
- Transcendence ("I'm beyond this tension")

**What containment IS:**
- Recognition (this is structural, expected)
- Sequential choice (autonomy today, connection tomorrow)
- Partial satisfaction (both get some attention)
- Mature navigation (comfort with incompletion)

**The strategic benefit:** Containment prevents collapse from forced resolution. You maintain both goods through alternation rather than destroying both through impossible simultaneous optimization.

**The failure mode:** Treating every tension as resolvable (exhaustion from impossible optimization) or treating everything as paradox (paralysis from excessive acceptance).

**Why this is conditional:** Some value resolution over acceptance, prefer clear answers over held tensions, or believe all contradictions can be transcended. The framework doesn't claim containment is universally better than resolution attempts.

**This is wisdom virtue.** Maturity is comfort with structural incompletion. Some things cannot be solved, only navigated.

## III. The Honest Limitations

**This ethics cannot tell you:**
- Which values to adopt (examined belief is your responsibility)
- Whether to prioritize agency, autonomy, coordination, adaptation, or maturity
- How much risk to accept when uncertain about classification
- When reform is worth the cost vs. exit or acceptance

**This ethics can tell you:**
- IF you hold these values, THEN these practices serve them
- How to check consistency between stated values and actual constraint-beliefs
- What strategic consequences follow from misclassification
- How different ethical frameworks navigate same classified constraints

**The relativism boundary:** Classification is indexed but objective. Ethics is values-dependent but not arbitrary. Your values determine response, but classification accuracy is still testable.

**The incompleteness:** Some constraints resist classification. Some situations involve multiple overlapping constraints. Some decisions require judgment calls this framework cannot resolve.

**The power limitation:** Even with perfect classification and clear values, you may lack power to act. Knowledge ≠ capability. This is not ethical failure; it's strategic constraint.

## IV. Indexed Obligation: How Different Values Navigate Same Constraints

**Same constraint, different ethical responses based on values:**

### Example: Carbon Credits (classified as Tangled Rope from moderate/biographical/constrained/national)

**Reformist (values coordination + justice):**
- Obligation: Work to preserve price signal coordination while cutting financial rent extraction
- Method: Policy advocacy for reformed carbon tax with progressive distribution

**Revolutionary (values radical change over incremental reform):**
- Obligation: Dismantle system, replace with direct regulation
- Method: Political mobilization against market-based solutions

**Conservative (values stability over perfect justice):**
- Obligation: Accept extraction as necessary cost of climate coordination
- Method: Participate in current system despite imperfections

**Libertarian (values individual freedom):**
- Obligation: Exit system if possible
- Method: Reduce carbon footprint voluntarily, avoid regulatory capture

**Pragmatist (values outcomes over principles):**
- Obligation: Use existing system while building better alternative
- Method: Participate now, fund experiments for replacement

**All are ethically coherent given their values.** Framework provides classification; values determine response.

### Example: Sleep Requirement (classified as Mountain from all indices)

**Health-first (values longevity):**
- Obligation: Defer immediately, protect sleep as non-negotiable
- Method: Structure life around sleep requirements

**Achievement-first (values accomplishment):**
- Obligation: Minimize sleep to biological threshold
- Method: Accept damage risk for productivity gains

**Balanced (values both health and productivity):**
- Obligation: Navigate strategically around constraint
- Method: Optimize sleep quality, work with circadian rhythm

**All are ethically coherent given their values.** Mountain classification doesn't dictate which navigation strategy is "correct."

## V. Integration: How the Ethics Work Together

The virtues are not contradictory but complementary. Effective agents need:

1. **Acceptance** - Identify what cannot be changed
2. **Resistance** - Reject extraction masquerading as necessity
3. **Reform** - Preserve coordination while removing extraction
4. **Construction** - Build new coordinations that don't extract
5. **Maintenance** - Remove obsolete structures
6. **Containment** - Hold irresolvable tensions

**The sequence in practice:**

1. Classify constraint accurately (epistemology)
2. Examine what you actually believe about it (foundation)
3. Determine which virtue applies based on type + values:
   - Mountain → Acceptance
   - Rope → Construction/Maintenance
   - Tangled Rope → Reform
   - Snare → Resistance
   - Piton → Maintenance (elimination)
   - Structural Paradox → Containment
4. Act according to power position (indexed obligation)
5. Monitor for misclassification and degradation
6. Revise as evidence accumulates

**The wisdom:** Know when to accept, resist, reform, build, remove, or contain. Applying wrong virtue to wrong constraint wastes energy or perpetuates harm.

## VI. The Core Ethical Commitment: Examined Belief

The framework's only universal ethical claim: **Examine your actual beliefs about constraints.**

Not what you wish you believed. Not what sounds impressive. Not what authority declares. What you actually, honestly believe about what constrains you.

**Why this matters:**

1. **Arguments only bind you if they rest on axioms you accept**
2. **Misalignment between stated and actual beliefs causes suffering**
3. **Strategic action requires accurate model of reality**
4. **Honesty about belief enables honest action**

**The practice:**
- When confronted with constraint claim, ask: "Do I actually believe this is unchangeable?"
- When defending constraint, ask: "Do I defend this because I believe it's necessary or because it serves me?"
- When challenging constraint, ask: "Do I challenge this because I believe it's fraudulent or because I dislike it?"
- Distinguish belief from preference, reality from desire

**The cost of unexamined belief:**
- Fighting Mountains you've misclassified as Snares (catastrophic failure)
- Accepting Snares you've misclassified as Mountains (unwarranted surrender)
- Defending Tangled Ropes as pure Ropes (complicity in extraction)
- Destroying Tangled Ropes as pure Snares (loss of coordination)

**The benefit of examined belief:**
- Strategic energy allocation
- Reduced cognitive dissonance
- Honest engagement with reality
- Effective action within actual constraints

## VII. What This Ethics Does and Doesn't Claim

**Does claim:**
- Classification accuracy has ethical significance (misclassification has consequences)
- Examined belief is ethically necessary (know what you actually believe)
- Conditional virtues serve specific values (IF value X, THEN practice Y)
- Indexed obligation makes sense (response depends on power position)

**Does NOT claim:**
- Universal moral truths (ethics is values-dependent)
- One correct set of values (different people prioritize differently)
- Classification determines obligation (values determine response)
- Ethical certainty (some situations resist clear answers)

**The meta-ethical position:**
- **Not moral realism** (no claim about objective moral facts)
- **Not relativism** (constraint classification is indexed but objective)
- **Not nihilism** (values matter even if not universal)
- **Conditional virtue ethics** (virtues serve values, both are choosable)

## VIII. Personal Audit Protocol

**For those wanting to examine their own constraint-beliefs:**

### Step 1: List Constraints You Face

Write down constraints you encounter regularly (work policies, social expectations, personal limits, institutional rules, biological needs, etc.)

### Step 2: Classify Each Constraint

Use classification tests from `field_manual.md`:
- Reality enforcement → Mountain
- Coordination + low extraction → Rope
- Coordination + high extraction → Tangled Rope
- High extraction, power-maintained → Snare
- Temporary with sunset → Scaffold
- High cost, no benefit → Piton

### Step 3: Check Your Actual Response

How do you actually treat this constraint? (Not how you think you should, how you do)
- Accept and navigate around?
- Maintain and refine?
- Try to reform?
- Resist and exit?
- Use temporarily?
- Bypass and eliminate?

### Step 4: Identify Misalignment

Where does your classification not match your response?
- Classified as Mountain but resist → Wasting energy or misclassified?
- Classified as Snare but accept → Surrendering or misclassified?
- Classified as Tangled Rope but treat as pure → Mishandling hybrid?

### Step 5: Test Your Belief

For each misalignment:
- Do I actually believe this classification?
- What evidence supports/contradicts it?
- Am I defending comfortable belief over accurate one?
- What would change my mind?

### Step 6: Align or Reclassify

Either:
- Change response to match accurate classification, or
- Change classification to match evidence, then adjust response

**The goal:** Consistency between examined belief, accurate classification, and strategic action.

## IX. The Goal: Living Consistently With Examined Beliefs

The framework's ethical ideal: **Live according to constraints you actually believe in, not constraints you've been told to believe in.**

**What this looks like:**

**Mountains:** Accept reality, navigate skillfully, don't waste energy fighting physics

**Ropes:** Maintain coordination that serves, refine when imperfect, replace when better alternatives exist

**Tangled Ropes:** Reform with surgical precision—preserve coordination core, excise extraction layer

**Snares:** Resist when possible, exit when not, expose always, refuse complicity

**Scaffolds:** Use temporarily, dismantle when done, resist permanence drift

**Pitons:** Eliminate obsolete structures, redirect saved energy

**Paradoxes:** Contain irresolvable tensions, sequential alternation, comfort with incompletion

**The test:** Can you articulate why you classify each constraint the way you do? Can you defend your response as aligned with your examined beliefs? Can you revise when evidence changes?

**The honesty:** Sometimes you lack power. Sometimes you choose strategic compliance over authentic expression. Sometimes you don't know the answer. That's honest. What's dishonest is claiming Mountain when you mean Snare, or claiming Snare when you mean "I don't like this."

## X. For Those Who Don't Share These Values

**If you don't value effective agency, autonomy, coordination, adaptation, or maturity:**

This ethics may not serve you. That's fine.

**Alternative value hierarchies that might prioritize differently:**

- **Devotion over accomplishment** - May accept claimed Mountains without testing (faith virtue)
- **Hierarchy over autonomy** - May defend Snares as necessary order
- **Tradition over adaptation** - May maintain Pitons for continuity
- **Purity over reform** - May prefer destruction to compromise with Tangled Ropes
- **Transcendence over containment** - May believe all paradoxes are resolvable

**The framework's response:** Classification still works. But strategic implications differ. If you value hierarchy, identifying a Snare doesn't mean "resist" but rather "this serves order, maintain it." Different values, different ethics, same classification.

**The only universal claim:** Examine what you actually believe. Beyond that, you choose.
