## What This Document Does

**Sociology extends Deferential Realism's constraint ontology into social space.** Where psychology.md handles internal constraints, this document handles **external social constraints operating between agents**.

The core insight: **The same constraint can function as coordination (Rope) for one person and extraction (Snare) for another—both classifications are objectively true from their respective power positions.**

This isn't moral relativism. It's **indexed realism**: social truth is relative to position in social space, but each indexed claim is objectively verifiable.

**Unifying principles:**
- **Indexical specification** - Classification requires WHO/WHEN/WHERE/HOW_MUCH
- **Power asymmetry detection** - Same structure appears differently based on position
- **Energy conservation** - Finite relational capacity demands strategic allocation
- **Reality-alignment** - Honest classification of what you're doing in relationship
- **Hybrid recognition** - Most social structures coordinate AND extract (Tangled Ropes fundamental)

**Empirical grounding:** Framework tested on relationship scenarios, organizational dynamics, social coordination mechanisms. Tangled Rope prevalence: 36% of analyzed constraints show irreducible hybrid structure.

## I. The Central Problem: Relational Relativity

Consider: "Is monogamy a good relationship structure?"

**Person A** [powerless/biographical/trapped/regional]:
- **Mountain** - Appears unchangeable. Social punishment for violation too high. Can't imagine alternatives.

**Person B** [moderate/biographical/mobile/national]:  
- **Tangled Rope** - Coordinates expectations (Rope function) BUT suppresses alternatives (Snare layer). Needs reform, not abandonment.

**Person C** [institutional/generational/constrained/global]:
- **Rope** - Essential coordination preserving stability, family infrastructure. Functions well at scale when chosen.

**Person D** [analytical/generational/analytical/global]:
- **Context-dependent** - Functional when chosen, extractive when imposed. The constraint is coercion, not structure itself.

**All four are objectively true from their respective indices.**

The disagreement isn't about facts—it's about **indexical underspecification**. Without specifying WHO evaluates this, WHEN (time horizon), WHERE (exit options), and HOW MUCH (scope), the question collapses into equivocation.

**This is why:** Most relational conflicts aren't value disagreements. They're **unrecognized index differences**. One person experiences Mountain (unchangeable from their position). Another experiences Tangled Rope (sees both coordination and extraction from theirs). Both are describing the same structure accurately from different positions in social space.

## II. The Four Indices in Social Space

Every classification requires specifying:

### WHO - Agent Power

- `powerless`: Subject to others' choices, minimal negotiating power, high exit costs
- `moderate`: Some agency, can negotiate, moderate exit costs  
- `powerful`: High agency, strong negotiating position, low exit costs
- `collective`: Organized group, shared power resources
- `institutional`: Organization-level power, sets rules
- `analytical`: Outside observer mapping structure

**Why this matters:** Power determines whether you experience a norm as unchangeable (Mountain) or negotiable (Rope).

**Power modifiers in social space:**
```
π(powerless)     = 1.5   (extraction amplified)
π(moderate)      = 1.0   (baseline)
π(powerful)      = 0.6   (extraction reduced)
π(collective)    = 0.4   (shared burden reduces individual extraction)
π(institutional) = -0.2  (net beneficiary from structure)
```

### WHEN - Time Horizon

- `immediate`: Single interaction, no long-term consequences
- `biographical`: Months to years, personal relationship duration
- `generational`: Decades, family/community continuity  
- `historical`: Centuries, institutional patterns
- `civilizational`: Millennia, fundamental social patterns

**Why this matters:** What appears temporary (Scaffold) in immediate timeframe may be structural extraction across generations.

### WHERE - Exit Options

- `trapped`: Cannot leave (family, economic dependence, legal bonds)
- `constrained`: High cost to exit but possible
- `mobile`: Moderate cost to exit
- `arbitrage`: Can move freely between contexts
- `analytical`: Outside system entirely

**Why this matters:** Exit options determine whether you must accept extraction as Mountain or can refuse it as Snare.

### HOW MUCH - Spatial Scope

- `dyadic`: Two-person relationship
- `small_group`: 3-12 people (family, close friend group)
- `community`: 20-150 people (Dunbar limit)
- `organizational`: Hundreds to thousands
- `regional`: City/state level
- `national`: Country level
- `global`: Billions

**Why this matters:** Coordination that works at dyadic scale may become extractive at societal scale. Power-scaling effects change dramatically across scope.

## III. Social Constraint Ontology

### A. Mountains: Natural Social Limits

**Definition:** Constraints rooted in human biology, information theory, or coordination physics that persist independent of social agreement.

**Epistemic test:** If everyone stopped believing it, would the constraint still operate?

**Examples:**
- **Dunbar number** (~150 stable relationships) - Cognitive limit on relationship maintenance. You can't maintain deep relationships with 500 people regardless of desire.
- **Trust decay over distance** - Information asymmetry increases with network distance. Can't verify reputation at 10 degrees of separation.
- **Reciprocity requirement** - Long-term relationships require mutual benefit or they collapse naturally, not through punishment.
- **Emotional labor limits** - Finite capacity for empathy and emotional processing. Empathy fatigue is biological, not choice.
- **Communication bandwidth** - Limited by human processing capacity and memory constraints.
- **Coordination complexity** - Coordinating N people requires O(N²) communication channels (mathematical constraint)

**Relational signature:**
- Persists across all cultures and historical periods
- Violations produce natural consequences (relationship failure, coordination collapse), not social punishment
- No enforcement needed—reality enforces itself
- Scale-invariant or has natural scaling law (like Dunbar number)

**Strategic response:** **Defer.** Design social structures around these limits, don't fight them.

**Example classification:**
- **FOR individual** [powerless/biographical/trapped/dyadic]: Mountain - Can't maintain 150+ deep relationships
- **FOR organization** [institutional/generational/mobile/organizational]: Mountain - Must design hierarchy to route information around cognitive limits
- **FOR platform** [institutional/civilizational/analytical/global]: Mountain - Architecture must accommodate fundamental coordination constraints

**Common misclassification (False Summit):** Treating social norms (constructed) as Mountains (natural). 

**Detection:** Check if it varies across cultures or history. If yes, not a Mountain.
- "Men are naturally dominant" → Varies culturally, not Mountain (Snare)
- "Humans need social connection" → Universal, doesn't vary, is Mountain

### B. Ropes: Functional Coordination

**Definition:** Structures solving genuine coordination problems with mutual benefit and low extraction.

**Epistemic test:** Does it enable collective action that couldn't happen otherwise? Is benefit distribution roughly symmetric?

**Examples:**
- **Traffic rules** - Prevent collisions at intersections. Fairly distributed costs (everyone stops sometimes). Alternative (chaos) is worse for everyone.
- **Time zones** - Coordinate activity across geography. Everyone benefits from synchronized schedules.
- **Mutual aid networks** - Reciprocal support with balanced exchange. "I help you move, you help me paint."
- **Friendship reciprocity norms** - "You listen when I struggle, I listen when you struggle." Fair emotional labor exchange.
- **Open-source collaboration norms** - Enable voluntary contribution without coercion. Benefits distributed to all users.
- **Shared calendaring** - Coordinates schedules, everyone benefits from reduced friction

**Relational signature:**
- Voluntary participation (in principle, though power affects access)
- Mutual benefit (everyone involved gains more than they give)
- Low enforcement requirement (people maintain it because it works)
- Alternatives exist but this solution is efficient
- Minimal suppression of other coordination mechanisms
- Base extraction ε(C) ≤ 0.15 (reciprocal exchange)

**Strategic response:** **Maintain.** Protect functional coordination. Reform if demonstrably better alternatives emerge. Monitor for degradation (Rope → Tangled Rope).

**Example classification:**
- **FOR both friends** [moderate/biographical/mobile/dyadic]: Rope - Coordinates emotional support, fair exchange
- **FOR one-sided relationship** [powerless/biographical/trapped/dyadic]: Not Rope - One person gives, other takes (Snare)
- **FOR community** [institutional/generational/constrained/community]: Rope - Pattern sustains mutual aid networks

**Degradation path:** Rope → Tangled Rope (extraction accumulates) → Piton (coordination function lost)

**Common confusion:** Confusing "works for me" with "works for everyone." A structure can be Rope for those with power and Snare for those without (power-scaling effect).

### C. Tangled Ropes: Hybrid Coordination-Extraction

**Definition:** Structures with genuine coordination function BUT implementation has asymmetric extraction. **Fundamental category in modern social life—36% of analyzed constraints.**

**Epistemic test:** Does it solve real coordination problem (Rope core)? Does it also extract asymmetrically (Snare layer)? Are both true simultaneously?

**Examples:**
- **Monogamy norms** - Coordinates expectations, reduces STI transmission, clarifies parenting responsibility (Rope function) BUT suppresses alternative relationship structures, enforces gender roles, shames non-conformity (Snare layer)
- **Professional networking** - Enables opportunity discovery, information sharing, mentorship access (Rope function) BUT privileges those with existing social capital, excludes outsiders, creates insider/outsider dynamics (Snare layer)
- **Family caregiving expectations** - Ensures elder care happens, provides intergenerational connection (Rope function) BUT extracts unpaid labor disproportionately from women, creates economic burden without support (Snare layer)
- **Dating market conventions** - Reduces search costs, coordinates expectations about interaction (Rope function) BUT enforces looks-based sorting, age discrimination, wealth signaling (Snare layer)
- **Workplace "culture fit"** - Enables team cohesion, shared values (Rope function) BUT excludes diversity, enforces conformity, gatekeeps opportunities (Snare layer)

**Relational signature:**
- Genuine coordination function demonstrably exists (can't dismiss as pure extraction)
- Asymmetric benefit distribution clearly present (can't dismiss as pure coordination)
- Requires enforcement for extraction layer (suppression of alternatives)
- Alternative structures viable but suppressed through shame, economics, or regulation
- Moderate extraction: 0.40 ≤ ε(C) ≤ 0.90 (between pure Rope and pure Snare)

**Power-scaling effect:**

Same base structure experienced differently by power position:

```
Monogamy norms, base extraction ε = 0.40

Powerless agent: χ(C) = 0.40 × 1.5 = 0.60 (Snare threshold - feels coercive)
Moderate agent: χ(C) = 0.40 × 1.0 = 0.40 (Tangled Rope - sees both functions)
Powerful agent: χ(C) = 0.40 × 0.6 = 0.24 (Rope territory - benefits from coordination)
```

This explains why same relationship structure IS Mountain/Tangled Rope/Rope/Snare from different indices—not perspectivism but structural relativity.

**Strategic response:** **Reform.** Preserve coordination function, excise extraction mechanism. Don't throw out coordination with the extraction.

**Example classification:**
- **FOR person with strong network** [powerful/biographical/mobile/organizational]: Rope - Enables job opportunities, collaboration, mentorship
- **FOR person without network** [powerless/biographical/trapped/organizational]: Snare - Locked out of opportunities, must build from scratch with no capital
- **FOR HR professional** [institutional/biographical/mobile/organizational]: Tangled Rope - Recognizes both coordination value AND inequity of access

**Why this matters:** Most real-world social structures are Tangled Ropes, not pure types. Failing to see both layers leads to:
- **Defending extraction** because you see coordination ("networking works!")
- **Destroying coordination** because you see extraction ("burn it all down!")
- **Better response:** Reform to preserve what works while removing what extracts

**Reform examples:**
- **Monogamy:** Keep coordination benefits for those who want them, remove shaming of alternatives, eliminate gendered role enforcement
- **Networking:** Keep information sharing, add equitable access programs, reduce insider advantage
- **Caregiving:** Keep intergenerational support, add economic support, redistribute labor more fairly
- **Dating conventions:** Keep search cost reduction, remove discrimination, expand valid partner criteria

**Common error:** Treating as pure Rope or pure Snare. Reality: Most structures have both layers. Nuanced response required.

**Terminology note:** This is the **most important category for modern social analysis**. Historical sociology often forced choice between "functional" (Durkheim) or "exploitative" (Marx). Tangled Ropes are both simultaneously—requires new analytical approach.

### D. Snares: Extractive Structures

**Definition:** Constraints maintained through power that asymmetrically benefit some at others' expense, often masquerading as Mountains (natural) or Ropes (coordination).

**Epistemic test:** Who benefits? Who bears costs? Is enforcement required? What alternatives are suppressed?

**Examples:**
- **Unpaid emotional labor expectations** - One party expected to manage relationships, remember dates, maintain social ties without reciprocation. Benefits party freed from labor, extracts from party performing it.
- **Professional gaslighting** - "You're too sensitive" when raising legitimate concerns. Benefits party avoiding accountability, harms party with valid complaint.
- **Obligatory presence requirements** - Must attend family events despite personal harm, for others' comfort. Benefits those who want your presence, extracts from your wellbeing.
- **Social debt spirals** - Expected to reciprocate favors you never requested. Creates asymmetric obligation.
- **Attention economy dynamics** - Platforms designed for engagement maximization regardless of user wellbeing. Benefits platform owners, extracts user attention and data.
- **"Traditional" gender roles** - Claims natural necessity, actually power-maintained extraction
- **Exclusionary group norms** - "We've always done it this way" maintaining insider advantage

**Relational signature:**
- Asymmetric benefit distribution (clear beneficiaries vs. those bearing costs)
- Requires active enforcement (social punishment, guilt induction, narrative control)
- Alternative arrangements actively suppressed or delegitimized
- **Naturalization rhetoric** ("that's just how relationships work," "everyone does this," "it's natural")
- High exit costs maintained artificially through shame, economic dependence, or social isolation
- High extraction: ε(C) ≥ 0.46 (concentrated benefits)

**Strategic response:** **Exit** where possible. **Contain damage** where trapped. **Refuse** where you have power. **Expose** always.

**Example classification:**
- **FOR woman in traditional relationship** [powerless/biographical/trapped/dyadic]: Snare - Expected to perform emotional labor, manage household, remember everything
- **FOR man benefiting** [moderate/biographical/constrained/dyadic]: May not recognize structure - relationship "just works" without visible labor (power blindness)
- **FOR feminist analyst** [analytical/generational/analytical/societal]: Snare - Systemic extraction pattern across millions of relationships

**Critical distinction:** Not all discomfort is extraction. Legitimate boundaries can feel uncomfortable to both parties. 

**Test:** Is discomfort from asymmetric burden (Snare), or from mutual limitation (Rope)?

**Detection markers:**
1. **Check benefit flow** - Does value consistently flow one direction?
2. **Check enforcement** - What maintains this? Punishment? Guilt? Economic necessity?
3. **Check alternatives** - Are other arrangements forbidden, shamed, or made economically impossible?
4. **Check naturalization** - Is it claimed as "just natural" or "just how things are"?
5. **Check snap-back** - If enforcement stopped, would structure dissolve quickly?

**Common misclassification (Type III Error):** Treating all relationship demands as extraction. Some coordination requires mutual sacrifice. Distinguish: equal burden (Rope) vs. asymmetric burden (Snare).

**Terminology note:** Previously "Noose" (execution device). "Snare" better fits climbing metaphor (trap in terrain) while maintaining the "tightening trap" concept.

### E. Scaffolds: Temporary Support

**Definition:** Coordination structures needed temporarily during transition, designed for dissolution.

**Epistemic test:** Is there natural completion point? Does success mean elimination? Is temporality explicit?

**Examples:**
- **Mentor-mentee relationship** - Support during skill acquisition, decays as mentee becomes peer
- **Support group** - Temporary community during crisis, healthy to graduate from
- **Transition rituals** - Mark passage (marriage, graduation, mourning), then dissolve
- **Crisis coordination** - Emergency response structures designed to disband when crisis ends
- **Onboarding programs** - Integrate new members, designed to end once integration complete
- **Scaffolded independence** - Parental support structures that dissolve as child develops autonomy

**Relational signature:**
- **Natural decay** without enforcement (relationship evolves as function served)
- **Success measured by elimination** (graduation is positive outcome)
- **Time-bound by design** (explicit or implicit sunset clause)
- **Support purpose** (provides capacity that will become internalized or unnecessary)
- **Intentional temporality** (both parties understand transience)

**Strategic response:** **Use deliberately, dismantle completely.** Don't convert temporary support into permanent dependence.

**Degradation paths:**
- **Scaffold → Piton:** Purpose served but not removed → bureaucratic/relational inertia
- **Scaffold → Tangled Rope:** Extraction added to temporary support → permanent rent-seeking (therapy dependency, mentorship exploitation)

**Hidden Tangled Rope risk:** Some "scaffolds" claim temporality but extract permanently:
- Mentorships that create dependency rather than independence
- Support groups that resist graduation to maintain membership
- Onboarding that becomes hazing or indefinite probation

**Example classification:**
- **FOR mentee** [powerless/biographical/constrained/dyadic]: Scaffold - Temporary learning support, designed to end
- **FOR mentor claiming permanence** [powerful/biographical/constrained/dyadic]: Tangled Rope or Snare - Extraction added to support function
- **FOR organization** [institutional/generational/mobile/organizational]: Scaffold - Onboarding program with clear completion criteria

**Exit planning in relationships:**
- Define completion criteria upfront
- Monitor for calcification
- Celebrate graduation, not mourn dissolution
- Prevent identity fusion with temporary role

### F. Pitons: Degraded Social Patterns

**Definition:** Social structures that once served coordination function but no longer do. High maintenance cost, no relational benefit.

**Epistemic test:** Does maintenance cost exceed benefit? Has function become obsolete? Does it persist through inertia rather than value?

**Examples:**
- **Outdated family rituals** - Holiday traditions that once bonded family, now just obligation everyone resents
- **Legacy social obligations** - "We always visit X" when relationship has no current value
- **Dead friendship maintenance** - Continuing contact out of habit when connection is gone
- **Vestigial professional networks** - LinkedIn connections maintained without function
- **Obsolete group memberships** - Clubs, organizations continued from inertia not value
- **Historical resentments** - Grudges maintained when original conflict is irrelevant
- **Empty social scripts** - "How are you?" / "Fine" exchanges with no communication function

**Relational signature:**
- **Historical function:** Once served genuine coordination or connection
- **Environmental shift:** Function became obsolete through life changes, geographic distance, development
- **Inertia maintenance:** Persists through habit, guilt, social expectation (not genuine value)
- **Resource waste:** Energy cost (time, emotional labor, coordination effort) exceeds any remaining benefit
- **No active defenders:** People tolerate rather than defend
- **Elimination relief:** When finally ended, all parties feel relief not loss

**Strategic response:** **Eliminate cleanly.** Release without guilt. Redirect saved relational energy to functional connections.

**Causal Profile:**
```
Historical necessity → Life transition → Functional obsolescence → Pattern persists
Example: College friendship (functional during school) → Geographic distance (transition) → No shared context (obsolescence) → Annual awkward coffee (Piton)
```

**Detection tests:**
- **Cost > benefit?** Does maintaining this relationship/ritual take more than it provides?
- **Environment shifted?** Did this serve a purpose in past life phase no longer present?
- **Better alternatives exist?** Could this relational energy be better allocated?
- **No one defends it?** Do participants maintain it out of obligation not desire?

**The clearing practice:**
- **Acknowledge what it was:** This served genuine function once
- **Recognize what it is:** Now obsolete, draining energy
- **Release without shame:** The pattern served its time, can be honored and released
- **Redirect energy:** Use freed capacity for functional relationships

**Example classification:**
- **FOR both parties** [moderate/biographical/mobile/dyadic]: Piton - Dead friendship both maintain from guilt, neither values
- **FOR one party holding on** [powerless/biographical/trapped/dyadic]: May be Snare for party forced to continue
- **FOR family** [moderate/generational/constrained/small_group]: Piton - Ritual everyone resents but no one stops

**Common resistance to elimination:**
- **Sunk cost fallacy:** "We've been friends for 20 years" (time invested doesn't make current pattern valuable)
- **Guilt induction:** "You're abandoning tradition/family/friends" (often from those benefiting from your labor)
- **Identity attachment:** "I've always been someone who..." (role no longer serves)

**The honest question:** "If this relationship/ritual/obligation disappeared tomorrow, would I feel relief or loss?"
- Relief → Piton, eliminate
- Loss → Still functional, maintain or reform

**Terminology note:** Previously "Zombie" constraints. "Piton" better fits climbing metaphor (old anchor that fails under load) and captures the danger: looks like support structure, wastes energy when maintained.

## IV. Detection Patterns

### A. False Summit (Naturalization)

**Pattern:** Social structure claimed as natural/unchangeable but actually constructed and maintained through power.

**Detection protocol:**

**Test 1: Cross-cultural variation**
- Does this "natural" pattern appear in all cultures?
- If it varies significantly, not a Mountain
- Example: "Women naturally care for children" → Varies by culture, not Mountain (Snare claiming Mountain status)

**Test 2: Historical variation**
- Has this "natural" pattern been different in past?
- If yes, not a Mountain
- Example: "Nuclear family is natural" → Recent invention (1950s), not Mountain

**Test 3: Enforcement check**
- What happens if you violate this "natural" pattern?
- Mountain violations produce reality failure (relationship collapse)
- Snare violations produce social punishment (shaming, ostracism)
- Example: "Monogamy is natural" → Violation produces social punishment not relationship failure (Snare)

**Test 4: Beneficiary analysis**
- Who benefits from claiming this is unchangeable?
- Mountains benefit no one specifically (apply equally)
- Snares concentrate benefits
- Example: "Gender roles are natural" → Benefits one gender systematically (Snare)

**Common False Summits in relationships:**
- "Men are naturally less emotional" (culturally constructed)
- "Women naturally want children" (socially enforced)
- "Jealousy is natural in love" (varies culturally)
- "Hierarchy is human nature" (many cultures function cooperatively)

**Why this matters:** Naturalizing Snares (calling them Mountains) enables extraction by making it seem inevitable. Critical feminist, anti-racist, and class analysis all involve False Summit detection.

### B. Hybrid Decomposition

**Pattern:** Structure appears monolithic but actually has separable coordination and extraction functions.

**Detection protocol:**

**Step 1: Identify claimed function**
- What problem does this supposedly solve?
- Is coordination function genuine or pretense?

**Step 2: Measure benefit distribution**
- Who gains from coordination function?
- Who gains from current implementation?
- Are these the same people?

**Step 3: Test separability**
- Can coordination persist without extraction?
- Do alternative implementations exist?
- What would minimal viable version look like?

**Example: Professional networking**
```
Coordination function: Information sharing, opportunity discovery, mentorship
Extraction mechanism: Insider advantage, exclusion of outsiders, social capital gatekeeping
Separable?: YES - can have information sharing without exclusion
Reform: Open networks, transparent opportunity posting, anti-nepotism policies
```

**Example: Family caregiving**
```
Coordination function: Ensure elders are cared for, intergenerational connection
Extraction mechanism: Unpaid labor from women, economic burden without support
Separable?: YES - can have elder care without gendered extraction
Reform: Economic support for caregivers, redistribute labor, professionalize when needed
```

### C. Power-Scaling Detection

**Pattern:** Same structure functions differently based on agent's power position.

**Detection protocol:**

**Step 1: Identify base structure**
- What is the constraint independent of power position?

**Step 2: Check experience variation**
- Do different agents report different experiences?
- Does someone with power say "works fine" while someone powerless says "oppressive"?

**Step 3: Calculate effective extraction**
- Base extraction ε(C)
- Agent power position P
- Effective extraction χ(C, P) = ε(C) × π(P)

**Step 4: Classify from each index**
- Powerless experience may be Snare
- Moderate experience may be Tangled Rope
- Powerful experience may be Rope
- **All are objectively true from their indices**

**Example: Workplace "open door policy"**
```
Base structure: Anyone can talk to leadership
Base extraction: ε = 0.35 (moderate - some gatekeeping exists)

Powerless employee: χ = 0.35 × 1.5 = 0.525 (Tangled Rope - fear of retaliation makes "open door" feel risky)
Moderate employee: χ = 0.35 × 1.0 = 0.35 (Tangled Rope - useful but selective)
Senior employee: χ = 0.35 × 0.6 = 0.21 (Rope - genuine access, low cost)

Same policy, three different experiences, all objectively true.
```

**Why this matters:** Explains relational conflicts. Partner with power experiences Rope, partner without power experiences Snare. Both are right. Problem is power asymmetry, not perception.

## V. Indexical Synchronization

### A. The Coordination Problem

**Two agents classifying same constraint from different indices:**

**Agent A** [powerless/biographical/trapped/dyadic]: Experiences as Snare
**Agent B** [powerful/biographical/mobile/dyadic]: Experiences as Rope

**Three response patterns:**

### 1. Asymmetric Index Assumption (Conflict)

Both assume their index is universal:
- A: "This is obviously exploitation, you're defending abuse"
- B: "This is obviously fair, you're being unreasonable"
- **Result:** Irreconcilable conflict, relationship damage

### 2. Index Recognition (Partial Resolution)

Acknowledge different structural positions:
- A: "From my position (trapped, powerless), this extracts. From yours (mobile, powerful), it coordinates."
- B: "I see how same structure functions differently for us."
- **Result:** Understanding but not necessarily resolution (power asymmetry remains)

### 3. Structural Reform (Full Resolution)

Address underlying power asymmetry or reform structure:
- Equalize power (increase A's exit options, reduce B's enforcement capacity)
- Reform structure (preserve coordination, reduce extraction)
- Exit structure (if reform impossible)
- **Result:** Actual change in constraint function

**The hard truth:** Index recognition alone doesn't change structure. If power asymmetry persists, A still experiences Snare, B still experiences Rope. Understanding is not liberation.

### B. Relationship Integrity Protocol

**When partners classify same constraint differently:**

**Step 1: Index Specification**
- A: State your WHO/WHEN/WHERE/HOW_MUCH
- B: State your WHO/WHEN/WHERE/HOW_MUCH
- Both: Acknowledge indices differ

**Step 2: Structural Analysis**
- What is base extraction ε(C)?
- How does power-scaling create different experiences?
- Is this Tangled Rope (both coordination AND extraction)?

**Step 3: Honest Classification**
- A: From my index, this is [type]
- B: From my index, this is [type]
- Both: These can both be true simultaneously

**Step 4: Response Decision**
- Can we equalize power?
- Can we reform structure?
- Can we accept different experiences?
- Should one/both of us exit?

**Example: Housework division**
```
A [powerless/biographical/trapped/dyadic]: "Division is unfair, I do 80% of invisible labor"
B [moderate/biographical/constrained/dyadic]: "We both contribute, just different tasks"

Step 1: Acknowledge A has less power (economic dependence, social pressure)
Step 2: Analyze structure - is division actually 80/20? What's invisible vs. visible labor?
Step 3: A experiences Snare (extraction), B experiences Rope (fair contribution)
Step 4: Options:
  - Equalize power (A gains economic independence)
  - Reform structure (redistribute labor more fairly)
  - Exit (separation if reform impossible)
```

## VI. Relational Energy & Boundaries

### A. Finite Relational Capacity

**Mountain:** Humans have limited relational energy (Dunbar number, emotional labor capacity, attention bandwidth).

**Implication:** Must allocate strategically. Not all relationships/obligations can be maintained.

**Energy allocation principle:**
- **Mountains:** Accept and work within limits
- **Ropes:** Maintain functional coordination
- **Tangled Ropes:** Reform or strategic engagement (high energy cost)
- **Snares:** Exit or contain damage (don't waste energy maintaining extraction)
- **Scaffolds:** Use temporarily, dismantle when done
- **Pitons:** Eliminate ruthlessly (pure energy waste)

### B. Boundary Types

**Boundary = constraint on interaction you impose**

**Type 1: Substrate Protection (Biological/Psychological Mountains)**
- "I need 8 hours sleep, won't commit to late-night calls"
- Function: Protect genuine limits
- Should be respected unconditionally

**Type 2: Coordination Boundary (Rope Maintenance)**
- "I'll respond to texts within 24 hours, not immediately"
- Function: Enable sustainable engagement
- Negotiable but serves both parties

**Type 3: Extraction Resistance (Snare Refusal)**
- "I won't manage your emotions for you"
- Function: Refuse asymmetric burden
- May be labeled "selfish" by beneficiaries

**Type 4: Temporary Boundary (Scaffold)**
- "During this crisis, I need extra space/support"
- Function: Manage transition
- Designed to dissolve when crisis passes

**Boundary violation patterns:**
- Treating Type 1 as negotiable → Substrate damage
- Treating Type 3 as selfishness → Enabling extraction
- Converting Type 4 to permanent → Calcification

## VII. Containment: Holding Paradox

**Some relational tensions are structurally irresolvable.** Forcing resolution causes relationship collapse.

### A. Structural Paradoxes in Social Life

**Autonomy ⇄ Connection**
- Both agents need independence AND intimacy
- Can't maximize both simultaneously
- Forcing resolution: isolation (autonomy) or fusion (connection)
- Containment: Sequential alternation (autonomy this week, connection next)

**Individual ⇄ Collective**
- Personal needs vs. group needs always in tension
- Can't perfectly satisfy both
- Forcing resolution: pure individualism or pure collectivism
- Containment: Context-dependent priority (personal needs in some contexts, collective in others)

**Honesty ⇄ Kindness**
- Sometimes truth hurts, kindness requires omission
- Can't always be both fully honest AND fully kind
- Forcing resolution: brutal honesty or polite dishonesty
- Containment: Skillful navigation (honest when it serves, kind when truth would only harm)

**Stability ⇄ Growth**
- Relationships need both security AND challenge
- Too much stability → stagnation, too much growth → chaos
- Forcing resolution: permanent comfort or constant disruption
- Containment: Rhythmic alternation (periods of stability, periods of growth)

### B. Containment Protocol (Relational)

**Six steps for navigating irresolvable tensions:**

**1. Name the paradox**
- What two goods are in tension?
- Are both genuinely valuable to relationship?

**2. Map the oscillation**
- How does tension manifest?
- What triggers swing toward each pole?

**3. Test edges**
- Is resolution actually impossible or just uncomfortable?
- What happens when we try to force resolution?
- Does relationship collapse under forced choice?

**4. Accept irresolvability**
- This is structural feature of relationship, not personal failure
- Neither pole can be eliminated without harm
- Tension is normal, expected, permanent

**5. Choose for current context**
- Which pole does this situation require?
- Partial satisfaction on one side now
- Trust other pole will have its turn

**6. Hold tension without collapse**
- Don't force simultaneous optimization ("perfect balance")
- Don't force resolution ("choose one forever")
- Sequential alternation across time
- Comfort with partial satisfaction on each pole

**Example: Autonomy ⇄ Connection in partnership**
```
Name: We both need independence AND closeness
Map: We swing between isolation (autonomy) and fusion (connection)
Test: Forcing "perfect balance every day" creates rigidity, neither satisfied
Accept: This tension is permanent feature of partnership
Choose: Autonomy priority this week (solo projects), connection next (quality time)
Hold: Neither is "winning" - both get partial satisfaction across time
```

**What containment is NOT:**
- Resignation ("nothing matters, give up")
- Optimization ("maximize both perfectly")
- Resolution ("I figured it out forever")
- Transcendence ("I'm beyond this tension")

**What containment IS:**
- Recognition (this is structural, not fixable)
- Sequential choice (context-dependent priority)
- Partial satisfaction (both poles across time)
- Mature navigation (comfort with incompletion)

## IX. Patterns at Scale

### A. Micro (Dyadic/Small Group)

- Easier to verify constraint type (direct observation)
- Power asymmetries more visible
- Reform more feasible (fewer agents to coordinate)
- Tangled Ropes can often be decomposed
- Exit costs lower

### B. Meso (Community/Organizational)

- Coordination benefits more apparent
- Power structures more complex
- Tangled Ropes more common (institutional hybrid structures)
- Reform requires collective action
- Exit costs moderate

### C. Macro (Regional/National/Global)

- Coordination essential at scale (traffic laws, time zones)
- Power asymmetries harder to detect (structural)
- Most constraints are Tangled Ropes (complex hybrid systems)
- Reform extraordinarily difficult (entrenched interests)
- Exit costs often prohibitive
- False Summits most common (naturalization at scale)

**Scale principle:** Larger scope generally increases:
- Tangled Rope prevalence (coordination + extraction both present)
- Detection difficulty (power concealment more sophisticated)
- Reform difficulty (more actors, more resistance)
- Naturalization rhetoric (claims of necessity intensify)

- Reform Tangled Ropes (preserve coordination, excise extraction)

- Eliminate Pitons ruthlessly (pure energy waste)
- Contain structural paradoxes (don't force resolution)

**The goal:** Navigate social constraint-space with integrity—seeing clearly what structures serve, what structures extract, and what structures do both. Honest classification of relational reality, not defense of comfortable illusions.
